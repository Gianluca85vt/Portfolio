In August 2025, Digital Foundry's John Linneman watched Elden Ring running on a
Switch 2 at Gamescom and called it simply unacceptable. Onlookers put the demo
somewhere around fifteen frames per second. So it was delayed.

It shipped on 28 August 2026. It is reviewing at 91.

That is the Metacritic figure as of 30 August, though the review count attached
to it moved from 31 to 40 over about forty-eight hours depending on when you
looked, which is normal for a launch week and worth stating rather than
pretending the number is settled. OpenCritic has it at 88 to 89 across roughly
37 critics, with every one of them recommending it.

## The six scores

Averaging the published verdicts that carry a number:

| Outlet | Score |
|---|---|
| Nintendo Life | 9 |
| RPGFan | 9.5 |
| Gamereactor UK | 9 |
| DualShockers | 9.5 |
| Meristation | 9.5 |
| TechRadar Gaming | 10 |

Mean of 9.42, which rounds to **9.5**. Empire filed without a number. It called
the port near faultless, which is not something arithmetic can use.

Six is a small sample against the forty-odd that exist, and the ones I could
reach skew toward Nintendo-focused and enthusiast outlets, which is a real
selection effect on a Switch 2 exclusive. Read the 9.5 as a reading of that
sample rather than the whole field.

## What nobody docked it for

Three days ago the observable story about this port was subtraction. Thinner
grass across Limgrave and the Altus Plateau, a 30fps target in both modes, a
year of somebody's life spent finding milliseconds inside the content.

The foliage barely comes up.

That is the genuinely interesting result. Reviewers noticed the compromises —
TheSixthAxis reports the 30fps target holding with more visible drops in
demanding moments, TechRadar hit pop-in and jerky enemy animation when the
screen filled up — but almost nobody translated those observations into a
lower number. The complaints that survived into the scoring are about load,
not about density.

Which is what a well-placed compromise looks like from the outside. Take a
third of the blades out of a field and, at riding speed, a field still reads as
a field. Let an enemy animation stutter while six of them close on you and the
player feels it in their hands immediately. Both cuts save frame time. Only one
of them costs you a point.

Porting is largely the craft of knowing which is which, and the gap between a
15fps demo and a 91 is a year of somebody making that call several thousand
times.

## The resolution numbers do not agree

This keeps getting reported as settled. It is not.

One set of write-ups has the game rendering from 1080p and reconstructing to
QHD. Another has it at 1080p in both handheld and docked. Coverage before
launch described 4K docked and 1080p handheld with DLSS doing the work. These
cannot all be true, and no first-party breakdown from FromSoftware or Bandai
Namco pins it down.

The figure every source does agree on is 30fps as the target in both modes,
with reconstruction rather than native rendering underneath. Until somebody
publishes a proper pixel count, that is the honest description.

## A great game, not the best version of it

Gamereactor's line is the one to hold onto: the Switch 2 is not the best place
to play Elden Ring for the first time, and that takes nothing away from being
able to play something this size on a train.

Nearly every review lands in that same shape. The 9s are for Elden Ring, which
was already a 96 in 2022 and has not changed. The port's own contribution is
that it does not get in the way — which sounds like faint praise and is
actually the entire brief when you are fitting a 2022 open world designed
around a mains socket into a 33ms budget you can hold.

The package is $79.99 with Shadow of the Erdtree included, plus two starting
classes and Torrent skins that also went to PlayStation, Xbox and PC the same
day as a separate pack. So the exclusive content stopped being exclusive
immediately, and what you are buying is the port.

On the evidence of six scores and a 91, the port is the good part.
