Gameplay footage from **Star Wars: Magellan**, the third-person action-RPG that
**Echtra Games** was building before Zynga shut the studio down in June 2025,
surfaced online this weekend. It comes from a former Echtra developer, roughly a
year after the project was officially cancelled, and it's the first real look
anyone outside the studio has had at what four-plus years of work on it actually
looked like in motion. Reported by [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/star-wars-magellan-footage-cancelled-action-rpg)
and covered in more detail by [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/canceled-star-wars-rpg-magellan-jedi-mandalorian/)
and [GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/action-rpg/canceled-star-wars-rpg-surfaces-1-year-after-it-was-officially-scrapped-revealing-jedi-and-mandalorian-combat-looter-shooter-gameplay-and-more-well-never-get-to-experience-ourselves/).

## What Magellan was

Echtra — founded by Diablo co-creator Max Schaefer, best known before this for
*Torchlight III* — had been building Magellan since early 2021, in **Unreal
Engine 5**, targeting PC, PS5 and Xbox Series X/S. It was a multiplayer
looter-shooter ARPG with two starting classes: a Jedi (codenamed Cleric, using
a lightsaber and Force powers) and a Mandalorian (codenamed Triggerman, blaster
and jetpack), with more weapons and Force abilities to unlock as loot. Levels
were built vertically, with hub areas players could explore between missions,
and an "NPC bond system" meant to tie relationships with non-playable
characters into how the story played out. Zynga acquired Echtra in 2021 and
closed it four years later without the studio ever shipping a single game.

That's the part worth sitting with before getting to the footage: this wasn't
a six-month prototype. It's the better part of half a decade of a full studio's
output, gone before a single public build existed.

## What four years of unreleased environment work looks like

The reporting on the leaked clips describes hub environments meant to be
explored outside combat — the kind of space that in a live production gets
revisited dozens of times across a project: greybox, first pass, lighting
pass, narrative dressing, then months of polish once the game's target
platforms and performance budget are actually locked. None of that late-stage
work happens on a level that's going to ship in six months. It happens on a
level a team believes it has years to get right, because until closure day,
that's exactly what Magellan's team had.

This is the detail that doesn't make it into most of the coverage, because
it's not a dramatic detail — it's a production detail. A live-service ARPG
with vertical hub levels and a loot-and-progression loop needs an enormous
amount of environment infrastructure just to function: modular kits built to
support random loot placement, lighting rigs that hold up across dozens of
mission variants, LOD and streaming budgets tuned for the largest space in the
game, not the smallest. That's infrastructure work, and it's exactly the kind
of work that produces nothing anyone outside the studio ever sees if the
project dies before a marketing beat requires a trailer. Echtra apparently
never got far enough to need one.

## Where it actually goes

Zynga has said nothing public about what happens to Magellan's assets, and
there's no reason to expect it will — engine content built under a Lucasfilm
license reverts to Lucasfilm's control on cancellation, and licensed IP work
essentially never gets salvaged into something else, unlike an original IP a
studio can sometimes shop around after a closure. The environments in that
footage are Star Wars locations that will not appear in any future Star Wars
game rebuilt from this pass, because a rebuild starts over. What's left is
what leaked: unpolished handheld footage of somebody's years of level and
lighting work, seen once, by an audience it was never made for.

It's worth remembering the shape of that loss the next time a big licensed
project gets cancelled and the headline is just a number of jobs. The jobs are
the real story. But so is the specific, unglamorous fact that a hub level's
lighting pass doesn't survive a studio closure any better than the studio
itself does.
