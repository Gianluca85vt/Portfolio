The sentence worth reading twice sits a long way down a press release dated
9 October. The concept and storytelling for *The Painted Soul*, Rakuten
Viki says, was developed by a team of creatives at Imaginus "who utilized AI
and other technologies."

Read it slowly. It is a careful sentence. Creatives developed the thing; AI was
among the tools they used; and the work described is concept and storytelling.
That is pre-production. Not animation, not design, not compositing.

The show itself premieres on 21 October — tomorrow — in the Americas, Europe,
the Middle East, Oceania, India and Southeast Asia, ten episodes, exclusively
on Viki. It is a fantasy romance. An apprentice spirit painter named Yun Hwa
breaks into a forbidden annex and wakes Seol Jin, a legendary painter sealed
inside a painting, and his curse turns out not to touch her. Viki's own release
says he was sealed for decades; the coverage that followed says fifty years in
one place and a century in another, which tells you how much of the plot
summary going around came from the release and how much came from somebody
watching the teaser.

## Where the AI sits

Concept and storytelling is the front of the pipeline, and it is the part of a
production where being wrong is survivable. A character who does not work on a
whiteboard gets redrawn on the whiteboard. A character who does not work in
episode four, after the model sheets are locked and the in-betweens are out at
a partner studio, gets shipped.

That is the whole reason this is the place a studio tries it first, and the
numbers elsewhere point the same way. CESA's 2026 survey of the Japanese
industry put generative-AI use at 85.8 per cent and [left visuals near the
bottom of the task list](/blog/cesa-2026-survey-generative-ai-japan/).
Adoption is close to universal. It lands away from the drawing.

What the release does not do is describe the animation. No technique, no
production breakdown, no partner studio. For a live-action drama that silence
would be ordinary. For an animated series the technique *is* the show — 2D on
threes reads nothing like cel-shaded 3D, and a viewer clocks the difference in
four seconds even if they could not name it. Headlines have already compressed
all of this into "AI-assisted Korean-animated series," which is a good deal more
than the release claims.

There is one place to go and look. Viki put a teaser up on 13 October, on
[its own page for the series](https://www.viki.com/videos/1273562v-trailer-1),
and until the premiere that is the whole visual record — no press stills, no
production gallery, no technique breakdown. A streamer with a pipeline question
hanging over its first animated commission has put out a teaser and a synopsis.

The gap between a studio's description of its own work and the label the
internet puts on it runs in both directions. Amplitude shot a *Humankind 2*
trailer at Nu Boyana with twenty-five actors and a hundred and fifty crew and
[got called AI slop for it anyway](/blog/humankind-2-trailer-ai-accusation-150-crew/).
By now the word mostly registers as a suspicion about how something was made.

## Who is making it

Imaginus arrives with a pedigree. The company was set up in December 2021 by Jinnie
Choi, who was Studio Dragon's inaugural CEO and ran it for five years — which
is to say she built the production machine behind the K-drama boom and then
left to start something with ten subsidiary labels under it. Its slate is
otherwise live action: *Tempest*, *Typhoon Company*, *Can This Love Be
Translated*.

Directing is Choi Junbae, credited on *Mouse* and *Stealer: The Treasure
Keeper*. Both live action. Moving a drama director onto an animated series is a
real decision and not a safe one — the timing instincts transfer, the shot
economics do not. In live action a reaction shot costs a camera setup. In
animation it costs a drawing, and the director who does not feel that in their
hands tends to write coverage nobody can afford to draw.

Viki's chief content officer Jaehee Hong said the platform plans to bring more
animation to the service and that this is "just the start," which is a
streamer's way of saying the pilot is the strategy.

## The other half of the week

Two days before the premiere, Lezhin Entertainment put Teaz into Korea on
19 October — an AI character chat service built on official webtoon IP from
Lezhin Comics and Bomtoon. Only Korean-language coverage of the launch has
surfaced so far, so treat the date as announced rather than witnessed. Users drop into a specific point in a story and talk
to the characters, taking a role or watching from outside, and the characters'
reactions shift with what you say.

Here is the part a working artist should notice. Teaz gamifies progress, and the
reward you unlock is hidden illustrations drawn by the original creators.

So in the same week, in the same industry, AI is doing the concept pass at one
company and the conversation at another — and at both, the drawn image is the
thing held back as valuable. The model generates the approach and the chatter.
The artist's hand is the payoff.

That is where it sits in October 2026, in two products shipping two days
apart. Both put the model where the work is still cheap to throw away, and both
keep a human drawing as the thing worth paying for.
