Xbox published the next stage of its restructure on 22 September. Two hundred
and sixty-eight roles go, across Halo Studios, other first-party teams and the
Xbox Game Studios management layer. Activision takes over the next Halo with a
purpose-built team while a small group stays behind to keep the live games
running. Rare moves under Activision too. Obsidian reports to Bethesda.
Playground Games and Turn 10 merge into one studio. The casual games group
folds into King.

And Ninja Theory enters consultation on a proposed closure, because two
separate agreements to sell it fell through.

That last line is the one that matters to anyone who works with a camera rig.

## What actually closes

A studio closing is usually reported as a back catalogue and a logo. Heavenly
Sword, Enslaved, DmC, Bleeding Edge, two Hellblades. All of that survives
perfectly well; the files are on a server in Redmond and somebody will
eventually put them on a sale page.

What does not survive is the pipeline, and Ninja Theory's was unusual.

In 2016 the studio put a live performance-capture demo on a stage — Senua's
face driven in real time inside Unreal, built with Epic, Cubic Motion and
3Lateral. At the time that was a research result, the kind of thing a
hundred-person studio in Cambridge had no business producing. By Hellblade II
in 2024 the same team was shipping photogrammetry of Icelandic basalt at a
density that made a lot of much larger productions look approximate.

Nobody sells that. It is a set of calibration habits, a rig that somebody
tuned over eight years, a solve that works because a particular
technical artist knows which forty blendshapes to fix by hand when the machine
gets the jaw wrong. That knowledge lives in people and in a room. Consultation
periods disperse both.

## The arithmetic of the reset

Booty's own framing is that this brings the process "roughly three-quarters"
of the way through. Hold that against the numbers Microsoft has published.
July's announcement was 1,600 people immediately and a further 1,600 across
the fiscal year ending 30 June 2027. Add the 268 and you are at 1,868 of
roughly 3,200.

Three-quarters is generous arithmetic on those figures. More than a thousand
jobs are still scheduled.

The Gears of War: E-Day story director was among the people cut this week, two
weeks before a game he had worked on for over four years ships. That timing
tells you the cuts are being made against a headcount target rather than a
production calendar, which is the detail worth carrying into any read of what
comes next.

## Where the good outcomes went

Three studios got out of the Xbox publishing arm intact this year. Double Fine
and Compulsion went independent and kept their IP and back catalogues. Undead
Labs kept State of Decay 3 alive, with the game still slated for Game Pass on
day one — studio head Philip Holt's own statement calls the studio
"independent, employee-owned", though some reports the same day described a
new unannounced owner instead. Worth watching which of those turns out to be
accurate.

Ninja Theory had two chances at that door and both closed. Under UK law a
proposed closure of this size triggers a consultation period of at least
thirty days, which is what "continuing to explore other paths forward" is
describing. It is a real window rather than a formality, and studios have been
bought inside one before.

[Build A Rocket Boy was reported to be closing after its own
cuts](/blog/build-a-rocket-boy-closing-mindseye-arcadia/) six days ago. Nobody
has stopped wanting games that look like this. The owners willing to carry the
fixed cost of a team that can make them are getting scarcer by the month.

## Practical note, for once

If you are an artist inside any of this, the boring advice is the advice that
keeps proving correct: get your reel, your breakdowns and your source files
somewhere you control, before the consultation ends and the licences get
revoked. [Zerply's shutdown made the same point three weeks
ago](/blog/zerply-closing-export-your-work/) and the deadline there was
similarly short.

Company laptops get collected. Perforce access gets revoked on a schedule
nobody tells you in advance. The screenshot you took on a Tuesday because it
looked nice is sometimes the only frame of four years of work you end up
owning.
