Production I.G and Khara opened the official site for Cosmoship Yamato √2199 on
6 October: a title, a staff list, one teaser visual. No cast, no runtime, no
release window. Khara first said it was making a Yamato film back in October
2024, so two years have bought a fairly thin page.

The staff list repays reading closely. Hideaki Anno directs, Yutaka Izubuchi
co-directs, and Anno holds the planning and executive producer credits too,
sharing the planning line with Mitsuhisa Ishikawa. Yoshinobu
Nishizaki, who co-created the 1974 original, holds the original work credit.
Then both directors are credited on design works.

That last credit is a pipeline decision wearing a credit's clothes.

## The ship is the lead

A battleship silhouette punishes every shortcut in a cel pipeline. Hundreds of
metres of panel line. A hull that has to hold its shape
while the camera crawls along it, because crawling along it is the franchise's
signature move, the shot the whole thing exists for. Cel-shaded hard surface at
that scale shows you where the normals break, where the line weight goes thin,
where somebody decided a seam could be a texture instead of geometry.

On a normal production that asset passes through three sets of hands. Mech
design draws the sheet. A modelling team builds from it, usually at a different
company. Layout frames whatever comes back. Each handoff is a chance for the
thing to stop reading like the drawing, and the director sees the result after
all three have happened.

Anno and Izubuchi holding both the direction and the design works puts two of
those hands in the same room. Whether that produces a better ship I have no
idea. It does mean nobody has to send a note downstream asking why the bow
looks wrong.

## Izubuchi already did this once, on a TV schedule

Izubuchi wrote and directed Space Battleship Yamato 2199 in 2012, co-directing
the television edition with Akihiro Enomoto, produced by AIC for the first ten
episodes and Xebec for the remaining sixteen. The ship CG was handled at a
separate company. Most of the in-fiction monitor graphics were built in Cinema
4D.

What 2199 did well was ration. CG led on the things CG is good at — monitor
displays, props, the strategy board, the workings of the Wave-Motion Engine —
and the character animation stayed on paper, with the integration tuned so a
hand-drawn figure could stand in a CG interior without the seam showing. That
is a discipline, and it is the kind of discipline a weekly schedule enforces
whether you want it or not.

A feature is graded and composited to a different standard, and it gets looked
at in a cinema by people who paid to sit close. The rationing that worked
across twenty-six television episodes is not automatically the right answer for
one film.

## Whose house look governs the hull

This is the first joint anime production between Production I.G and Khara, and
the two studios do not finish a frame the same way. I.G's recent science
fiction work leans into CG fleets and holds them there. The Evangelion rebuilds
that came out of Khara do something closer to the opposite: whatever the
geometry was doing in the viewport, the finished image is flattened hard enough
that you stop reading it as geometry.

Both approaches work. They produce different ships. No announcement says which
studio owns the Yamato asset, and the answer decides whether the hull arrives
as Izubuchi's drawing or as flattened geometry wearing his design. The same
question is why [ufotable's lighting carries so much of Witch on the Holy
Night](/blog/witch-on-the-holy-night-ufotable-photography/) — a house look is a
pipeline, and it shows up in every frame whether or not anyone names it in a
press release.

## The square root

The title is doing something. 2199 was Izubuchi's remake, and √2199 positions
this film as derived from that rather than from the 1974 series, even though
Nishizaki keeps the original work credit. Anno has reportedly said it takes a
different path from the remakes, which cuts against that reading, so treat the
maths in the title as my guess rather than anything confirmed.

What is not a guess is how long Anno has been circling this. He was fourteen
when the original aired in 1974 and has said he does not think his life would
have been the same without it. Daicon Film, the student outfit he made shorts
with before any of the rest of it, was packed with Yamato references. He
fronted the series' fiftieth anniversary exhibition last year. The man has been
waiting a while.

One honest gap: I have not seen the teaser visual, and the reports describing
it do not agree. One has the Yamato in silhouette against a wrecked landscape,
preparing to launch. Another has it under the Milky Way. Those are different
images with different things to say about the film, and until the site's own
asset is in front of me I am not going to pretend to read either one. The same
caution applies to anything about the surface treatment — a silhouette is the
safest first image a production like this can publish, because it commits to
nothing about how the hull will actually be shaded, which is the thing I want
to know and the thing [a cel scan gives up only under
magnification](/blog/ninja-scroll-4k-restoration-cels/).
