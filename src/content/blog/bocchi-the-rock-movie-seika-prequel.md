Sunday brought a Bocchi the Rock! film announcement, and it is not the one the
franchise has been making. The 2024 pair — *Re:* and *Re:Re:* — were recap
compilations, television footage cut back together for a theatre. This one is
subtitled *Hoshi no Ashiato*, it opens in Japanese cinemas in 2027, and it
adapts the manga's prologue story about Seika, Nijika's older sister. Maaya
Uchida plays Seika, Sayumi Suzushiro returns as Nijika. Shin Wakabayashi, who
directed Wonder Egg Priority, is directing.

That is a feature built out of original footage from a prologue chapter. It is
an expansion job, and the franchise has not done one before.

## What the style is for

If you have only heard about this show second-hand, you have heard about the
guitar. Fair enough — the performance animation is the part that makes other
artists sit up, because the hands are playing the chord you are hearing. That
is reference work, frame by frame, on shots most productions would cheat with a
wide angle and a cymbal flare.

But the thing the show is known for is the register switching. Hitori Gotoh
panics, and the frame stops being a drawing: claymation, cut paper, a
photograph, a crayon scribble, live-action footage spliced in for two seconds.
The switching does narration work. Every one of them is anchored to one girl's interior state, which is why they land
instead of feeling like a gag reel.

Seika is not that character. She runs the live house, she keeps her face still,
she is the adult in the room in a show full of teenagers who are not. A prequel
about her band days has no panic engine. So the film either finds a different
motivation for breaking the frame, or it plays straight and gives up the thing
people clip and repost. I would rather watch the first version, and I would not
want to be the one who has to pitch it.

## Three productions, three directors

Keiichirou Saitou directed the 2022 series at CloverWorks and set all of this.
Season 2, announced in February 2025, went to Yusuke Yamamoto, who had been
assistant director on season one — a handover inside the house. The film goes
to Wakabayashi, who was not on either.

That is a lot of movement for a show whose value is in a specific set of
choices made by a specific group of people. It is also the normal shape of
anime production, and not automatically bad: [Edgerunners 2 handing the whole
series to the director of its best
episode](/blog/edgerunners-2-ikarashi-series-director/) is the same manoeuvre
pointed in a good direction, and [Look Back putting its drawing in the hands of
a rotoscope specialist](/blog/look-back-live-action-rotoscope-sequence/)
produced something neither the manga nor a conventional adaptation would have.
A director arriving from outside brings a register. Wonder Egg Priority had a
dense, layout-forward one, and a schedule that famously came apart in public
before the end.

A feature is a kinder container for that. Fixed delivery, one deadline, more
hours per cut than a weekly episode gets, and no twelfth-week wall. If you were
going to give Wakabayashi a Bocchi production, the film is the one to give him.

## Small things worth noting

The outlets do not agree on how to spell the family name — ANN's headline has
Nijika Ichiji, MyAnimeList has Ijichi, which is the one that matches the
Japanese. Minor, but it tells you how fast everyone turned this around on a
Sunday.

And none of the write-ups I have read say what Seika played. In a film about a
band that is not a detail you leave to the trailer, so I expect it is being
held. If it is a drum kit, the animators have a harder job than the guitar
scenes ever gave them — four limbs, independent, visible, and no way to hide a
wrong hand behind a neck.
