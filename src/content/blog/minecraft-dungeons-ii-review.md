Reviews for Minecraft Dungeons II appeared on 28 September 2026, a day before
the game reaches PC, Xbox Series X|S, PS5, Switch and Switch 2 at €29.99, day
one on Game Pass. Mojang Studios and Double Eleven built it, Xbox Game Studios
is publishing. A day-before embargo is the ordinary shape of the thing, so
nobody was hiding anything.

The line I keep rereading is Game Informer's, about the added jump making
traversal more fluid. Six words, and behind them sits months of somebody's
work.

## The first game could not jump

Minecraft Dungeons shipped in 2020 with a roll and no jump button. The Get

split armour slots, talismans and a new dimension called the Sift.

Build a crawler without a jump and you get to author levels as a navigable
plane. Height becomes a wall. A one-block ledge is as impassable as a cliff,
and it costs nothing to place, so designers place thousands of them — as
funnels, as arena edges, as the thing that stops a player skipping the
encounter you built. The navmesh and the art agree with each other because
there was only ever one answer to "can I go up there".

Add a jump and every one of those ledges becomes a question. Climbable, or
blocked? If it is blocked, something has to say so — an invisible collider, a
taller lip, a piece of set dressing that reads as a barrier from the camera
distance the game actually uses. Do that pass badly and players hop onto roofs
and out of arenas. Do it invisibly and they bounce off geometry that looks
exactly like the geometry they just climbed. Neither is subtle when it goes
wrong.

Which is why two findings in this review cycle read to me as the same finding.
Game Informer says traversal is more fluid. Neowin says bugs and glitches kept
its score down. A retrofit that mostly worked, with a tail of cases that did
not, is what a jump costs in a game that was not designed around one.

## Three numbers, and I am saying so

Across the verdicts published by the time I collected them on 28 September,
three carried a number I could verify: Game Informer at 7.25, DualShockers at
7.5, Loot Level Chill at 8.5. The plain mean is 7.75, which rounds to **8**.

That is thin and I would rather label it than dress it up. Around ten outlets
had filed by then — GamesRadar+, Shacknews, TheSixthAxis, Checkpoint, Neowin,
AltChar, Worthplaying, Game8 among them — and most either score on a scale I
could not confirm from outside their paywall-free snippets or do not attach a
number at all. Metacritic had not aggregated critics yet. Come back in a week
and the average will have moved.

The spread across the three is 1.25 points, which is narrow, and the written
verdicts are wider than that suggests. GamesRadar+ calls it one of the
best-made loot crawlers you can buy and notes how well it opens up to younger
players. Checkpoint filed under the headline "Too deep, too greedily" and
argues that bigger has not produced better. Those two are not 1.25 points
apart in spirit.

## The complaint that recurs

Shacknews put it in the title: enjoyable with friends, meaning local ones, not
online ones. Neowin's bugs. Checkpoint's greed. Three different outlets landing
on the state of the thing rather than the design of it.

This blog has been here recently with
[a good football game carrying one structural complaint reviewers kept returning to](/blog/ea-sports-fc-27-review/) —
six verdicts averaging 7.5 and every one of them circling the same addition.
The pattern repeats because it is the one thing a review cycle is good at
catching. Systems get judged against the last game in the series. Launch state
gets judged against nothing, and reviewers simply report what happened to them
that week.

Worth remembering that a launch-state complaint is the most fixable kind and
also the one that sets the Steam review tone for a year.

## Five SKUs on one Tuesday

PC, Xbox Series X|S, PS5, Switch and Switch 2, all on 29 September. Two of
those are Switches, which is the part that makes an art department sigh.

I wrote earlier about
[what porting Diablo 4 down to Switch 2 costs the art team](/blog/diablo-4-switch-2-age-of-hatred-what-porting-costs-art/),
and the arithmetic does not change for a blockier game. Fewer unique materials
and a fixed palette genuinely help — that is a real advantage of this art
style, not a consolation. But four players, a loot fountain and a new dimension
full of VFX still have to fit in a handheld memory budget, and the Sift is new
content authored after the rest of the game's targets were set. Shipping all
five on the same day means the last studio to finish sets the date for
everybody.
