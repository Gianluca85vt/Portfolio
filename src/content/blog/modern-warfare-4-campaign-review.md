Reviews for Modern Warfare 4 went up on 16 October, a week before the game
comes out, and every one of them covers about a third of it. Activision gave
digital pre-orders early access to the campaign and nothing else. Multiplayer
and DMZ arrive with the full release on 23 October, which is why most of the
outlets that filed last Friday filed without a number.

Five of them did put a number on the campaign. GameSpot 7, IGN 8 — tagged as
not final — But Why Tho? 8.5, Insider Gaming 9, and GamesBeat's reviewer at
4.5 out of 5, which is 9 on the scale everyone else uses. Mean of the five:
**8.5**. Range: 7 to 9.

Two points of spread on sixteen missions that take six to nine hours. That
is narrow by this franchise's standards, and the reviews agree on the reason —
the story missions are the best Infinity Ward has shipped since Modern Warfare
2019. North Korea invades the South, a South Korean squad holds a collapsing
front, and Price runs an off-book revenge war somewhere else entirely.

## The two points sit on one mission

Where the five diverge is a semi-open, behind-enemy-lines mission borrowed from
DMZ. IGN calls it a real highlight. GamesBeat says the open stretch is where
the pacing goes. GamesRadar, filing without a score, lands somewhere between
the two and blames rigid mission design around it rather than the mission
itself. A reader of all three comes away knowing the section exists and not
much else.

From the outside the disagreement reads like a budget, not a taste. A linear
Call Of Duty mission is authored for one route. The art team knows where the
camera will be, which means every silhouette, every light and every piece of
set dressing can be placed for that read and nothing else — and the moment the
player can approach from any direction, that stops being true. You get a kit
instead: modular buildings, instanced props, lighting that has to work from all
sides. Cheaper per square kilometre and thinner per minute. Players feel it as
pacing.

The blog made this exact argument earlier this month, when
[Gears of War: E-Day turned out to be best when it stays linear](/blog/gears-of-war-e-day-review/)
— also on campaign-only early reviews, also split on level structure, also
scoring an 8 off a wide range. Two shooters in a month is a pattern worth
naming. Linear campaigns are expensive and reviewers keep rewarding them, and
the open section is where the money ran out.

## The floor moved

The more interesting number is not the score. Modern Warfare 4 is the first
Modern Warfare that skips PS4 and Xbox One entirely, and Warzone left those
machines in June. For thirteen years the baseline for a Call of Duty art
budget was a Jaguar CPU and about 5GB of usable memory, because the campaign
had to run there too. It does not any more.

What replaces it as the low end is Switch 2, which ships day one — the first
Call of Duty on a Nintendo machine since Ghosts on Wii U, and the first time
Microsoft's regulatory promise has actually produced a game, after Black Ops 7
shipped without a Switch version at all. Infinity Ward is doing that build
itself, with support from Digital Legends, and Beenox has the PC side.

That is a different proposition to a port handed to an outside team after the
fact. We covered [what porting Diablo 4 to Switch 2 costs the art team](/blog/diablo-4-switch-2-age-of-hatred-what-porting-costs-art/)
— re-authored LODs, texture budgets cut and recut, lighting rebuilt for a
handheld thermal envelope. Doing it in-house, in parallel, means the decisions
land in the source assets rather than in a fork of them. Whether it worked is a
question for Digital Foundry next week. Nobody has measured the Switch 2 build
yet, and I am not going to guess at it.

## The number, and what it covers

**8.5**, from five published campaign scores between 7 and 9, collected on
19 October 2026 and converted to a ten-point scale.

Treat it as provisional in a way most averages are not. IGN's 8 carries its
own "score not final" tag, and one roundup reports no score on IGN's page at
all; the average is 8.5 with it and 8.5 without. More to the point, none of
these five has touched the multiplayer, and multiplayer is the part people
still play in March. The scores say the campaign is worth the week it takes to
finish. They say nothing yet about the game.
