Netflix has closed **Night School Studio**, the team behind *Oxenfree*, *Oxenfree II:
Lost Signals* and *Afterparty*, along with the Helsinki studio **Moonloot**. The
closures were first reported by Game File on 13 August and confirmed by outlets
including [GameDeveloper.com](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/netflix-closing-oxenfree-developer-night-school-studio-and-moonloot-games),
[PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/netflix-closes-oxenfree-developer-night-school-just-a-month-after-praising-its-new-game-as-one-of-the-companys-most-successful-cloud-game-debuts/)
and [Video Games Chronicle](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/netflix-is-shutting-down-oxenfree-studio-night-school-six-weeks-after-its-last-game-was-released/).
Netflix has not said how many people are affected.

The timing is the part that stings. Night School shipped *Unhinged*, a
first-person horror game built for Netflix's cloud-streamed TV app, six weeks
ago — and Netflix itself called it one of the platform's most successful cloud
game debuts a month before shutting the studio down. In its statement, Netflix
said it's narrowing its games focus to four lanes: kids' games, party games,
story-driven titles and games with "mainstream hooks," name-checking its recent
FIFA release. Night School, on paper, fit the "story-driven" bucket. It got cut
anyway.

I'm not going to pretend to have inside knowledge of Netflix's games strategy —
nobody outside the company does right now, and the reporting is honest about
that. What I do know something about is what gets lost when a studio like this
one closes, because Oxenfree's backgrounds are a genuine reference point for
environment art, and it's worth saying why while the reason people cared is
still fresh.

## The trick was never having real depth

  <figcaption>Oxenfree screenshot, Night School Studio, via the game's official Steam page</figcaption>
</figure>

*Oxenfree* looks like it has a painted 3D world. It doesn't. Art lead **Heather
Gross** built it as layered 2D parallax — flat painted planes stacked and moved
at different speeds against the camera, the way old Disney animated
backgrounds faked depth before anyone had a z-axis to spend. [DigiPen's writeup
on Gross's process](https://www.digipen.edu/showcase/news/digipen-graduate-heather-gross-art-lead-oxenfree-winning-awards-working-indie-games-whats-next)
and the game's own IGF materials describe the same pipeline: pencil sketches
on paper, scanned into Photoshop, refined into layered mattes, then composited
so the island of Edwards Island drifts past in believable depth as the camera
tracks the characters walking along it.

That's the part I'd flag to anyone doing environment work today, three-D or
otherwise. A fully modeled, physically-based 3D island would have cost this
small a team months it didn't have. Gross's parallax mattes got a comparable
emotional read — fog banks receding, cliffs looming, a lighthouse sitting just
slightly wrong on the horizon — for a fraction of the budget, because the
technique never tried to be more real than the story needed. *Oxenfree* won
the IGF Award for Excellence in Visual Art in 2016 on the strength of exactly
that restraint, not on polygon count.

It's a useful thing to sit with whenever a client or a lead asks for "more
detail." Sometimes the answer that actually reads better on screen is fewer,
better-composed layers, not more geometry.

## Restructuring is not a verdict on the work

Night School wasn't a struggling studio propped up past its usefulness.
Netflix acquired it in 2021 specifically because *Oxenfree* had built a
reputation few small teams manage, and the studio kept working after the
acquisition — a sequel, a *Black Mirror* tie-in game called *Thronglets*, and
*Unhinged* just six weeks ago. This is a strategy pivot inside a company that
has spent several years unsure what its games division is actually for, not a
studio running out of ideas.

That distinction matters if you're the kind of artist who reads "studio
closes" and quietly wonders whether the work was the problem. It usually
isn't. Netflix's own statement points at portfolio focus, not quality — and a
studio getting praised for a strong cloud-game debut and shut down a month
later is not a studio that lost the room.

## What I'd actually take from this

**If Oxenfree's parallax approach is new to you, it's worth an afternoon.**
Play through even the opening beach scene watching the layers instead of the
dialogue. It's a masterclass in getting depth cheap, and cheap-but-considered
is a skill that ages better than any specific engine.

**Don't read a closure as a review of craft.** Night School's catalogue —
*Oxenfree*, *Oxenfree II*, *Afterparty*, *Thronglets* — is still there, still
good, and still worth studying regardless of what happens to the logo on the
splash screen. Studios end for financing and portfolio reasons far more often
than for running out of good ideas.

**Watch where the people land.** A studio like this closing usually means a
concentrated pocket of narrative-art and lighting talent becomes available at
once. If you're hiring for that kind of work, that's worth paying attention to
in the coming months, and if you're one of the people affected, the portfolio
you're sitting on is a strong one to be job-hunting with.
