Somewhere in Los Angeles there is an office that worked on nothing but
pedestrians. That is the detail from last week's Rockstar press cycle that I
keep turning over — not the map size, not the eighty hours of story. An entire
building of people, for years, on the crowd.

The number attached to it is over 600,000 animations. Red Dead Redemption 2 is
put at around 300,000, and Grand Theft Auto 5 at roughly 55,000. Rockstar
North's Rob Nelson gave those figures during the hands-on sessions that ran
alongside the Extended Look, and the game is still dated **19 November 2026** on
PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

Then, separately, the other claim: no microtransactions, and no generative AI.

## Where the no-AI line came from

Worth being precise about this, because the headlines have been sloppy with it.
The confirmation did not arrive in a Rockstar press release. Andy Cortez and
Greg Miller of Kinda Funny went to Rockstar North in Edinburgh, put both
questions to Nelson in person, and reported his answers on their show
afterwards. Cortez's recounting was that he asked point blank whether AI was in
the game, and got a flat no.

That is a podcast host paraphrasing a conversation. It is credible, it has not
been disputed by Rockstar, and it is still second-hand.

The looser problem is what the claim covers. Some outlets have run it as "no
generative AI in the shipped game", others as "generative AI played no part in
development". Those are different statements about different things, and I
cannot see anything in the reporting that settles which one Nelson made. GTA has
had AI in the ordinary sense since the first 3D game — traffic that routes
itself, police that coordinate, pedestrians with a destination. Nobody is
claiming that has gone. What is being ruled out is the diffusion-and-transformer
kind, and the scope of the ruling-out is fuzzy.

## What a count of 600,000 is counting

Here is where I would push back on how the figure is being read. Six hundred
thousand animations does not mean six hundred thousand performances. It is a
count of clips in a database, and in a modern character system most of that
database is connective tissue.

Think about what one NPC needs to sit down at a table. There is the sit itself.
There is the approach, the turn, the hand on the chair back, the weight shift,
the settle. Then the same again for a chair on the left, a chair against a wall,
a stool, a booth. Then the additive layers that ride on top — breathing, a
glance, holding a drink, holding a phone, holding nothing. Then the exits, which
are not the entries played backwards. One believable interaction with one piece
of furniture can be dozens of entries in that count before anyone has acted a
line.

All of which makes it a labour number more than a spectacle
number. Somebody authored, retargeted, cleaned and hooked up every one
of those, and the count going up by a factor of ten since GTA 5 is the honest
measure of how the job has changed. Animators used to build a vocabulary. Now
they build a corpus.

There is a wrinkle in the comparison, too. The 300,000 figure for Red Dead 2 is
Rockstar's own, given now. At the time of that game's launch in 2018 the widely
repeated number was over half a million. Both cannot be right unless they are
counting different things, which is very likely what is happening — full
animation set versus NPC-only, or clips versus states. I would not build an
argument on either figure being exact.

## The two claims are the same claim

Hand-authoring at that volume and refusing generative tools are the same
position stated twice.

The obvious place a large studio would reach for generation is exactly this
work: the long tail of incidental motion, the barks, the filler that nobody
looks at directly but everybody notices the absence of. It is the least
glamorous content in the game and the most expensive per unit of player
attention. A studio that wanted to cut cost would cut it here first.

Rockstar did the opposite and staffed a building. Aaron Garbut described the
interiors in the same terms — who is sitting where, what they are drinking, the
marks left on the table, photographs on the wall showing the actual people who
live in that room. That is a payroll decision, and it shows up in the footage in
a way that spec sheets never do.

I have sat through enough of these showcases to be wary when a studio brands its
own restraint. "No generative AI" is a marketing line as much as a production
fact, deployed at a moment when saying it earns goodwill cheaply. But the
600,000 gives it weight. You cannot fake a corpus that size after the fact, and
a studio that had quietly generated half of it would be taking an enormous risk
by inviting the question in a room with a microphone in it.

What none of this touches is GTA Online. The no-monetisation answer was about
the single-player game shipping in November. Whatever comes later has its own
economics, and nobody asked Nelson about those.
