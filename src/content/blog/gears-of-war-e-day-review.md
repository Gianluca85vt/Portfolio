Reviews for Gears of War: E-Day went up on 1 October 2026. The game comes out
on the 6th. That gap is unusual enough on its own, and it got stranger when
the Guardian's review went live a day early, scored it 3 out of 5, and
vanished again — Notebookcheck and Power Up Gaming both caught it before it
came down.

Across the five scored verdicts I could verify and convert, the average is
**8**. The range runs from 6 to 9.5.

Three and a half points. On Onimusha a few weeks ago the spread was two, and
I said a tight spread usually means nobody is arguing about what the game was
trying to do. The inverse holds here.

## What nobody is arguing about

The shooting. Every outlet in the list, including the one that gave it a 6,
says the moment-to-moment combat is the best the series has produced. The
Guardian's own line, in the version that briefly went live, was that E-Day has
the best combat in twenty years of Gears.

So the 6 and the 9.5 are not disagreeing about whether the game is good at the
thing it is for. They are disagreeing about everything wrapped around it.

## The argument is about shape

PC Gamer filed under "good shooting, but the campaign's a slog". GameSpot's
Richard Wakeling called it a prequel with nothing new to say. GamesRadar
reached for Halo 3: ODST as the comparison and meant it as a compliment.
Eurogamer's Dom Peppiatt said it evolves the best of the 2006–2011 trilogy and
drops the worst. One review headline I found put it plainly: best Gears since
3, and brightest when it stays linear.

That last clause is the one worth sitting with from an art desk.

Gears of War built its identity on a very specific kind of space. A corridor
that opens into a courtyard, chest-high cover placed where a designer wanted
you to end up, a flank route that exists because somebody drew it. The cover
geography *is* the level design. It is furniture arranged on a stage, and the
arrangement is doing the work that a level-up system does in other games.

Widen that space and two things happen at once. The cover has to start looking
incidental — rubble, barricades, wrecked vehicles that read as debris rather
than as a designed fight — and the amount of authored detail you can afford per
square metre falls, because the metres multiply. CD PROJEKT RED's environment
team talked about [twice the texel density of The Witcher 3 in Night
City](/blog/night-city-hand-built-texel-density/), and were explicit that hand
placement was what made it possible. Hand placement scales with hands.

I have not played E-Day and will not pretend otherwise. What I can read is a
pattern: five outlets, no coordination, and the word that keeps surfacing for
the open sections is repetitive while the word for the tight ones is superb.
When independent reviewers converge on a complaint that maps that cleanly onto
a structural decision, the decision is usually the cause.

## Most of these reviews are incomplete

Worth saying loudly, because it changes what the number means. Reviewers
describe a review build with versus multiplayer and the Horde siege mode
switched off. That is why so many of these are filed as review-in-progress,
including GamesRadar's and PC Gamer's. Windows Central was explicit that its 8
was for the campaign alone.

So: an average built from five campaign verdicts, five days before anyone can
buy it, on a game whose multiplayer has historically been half the reason
people keep it installed. Treat the 8 as a campaign score with a label on it.

The aggregates were moving while I collected this. Metacritic read 88 and then
89 inside roughly a day, with OpenCritic at 87. Different snapshots, different
numbers — which is what an aggregate does when reviews are still landing.

DSOGaming benchmarked an RTX 5090 at roughly 67fps average at 4K Ultra with
DLSS 4.5 on Quality. Native 4K Ultra at 60 is, by their numbers, a 5090-only
proposition. TweakTown found a graphics preset called Ludicrous that drags even
that card down.

A "Ludicrous" setting is a studio being honest, and I would rather have it than
an Ultra preset that quietly costs the same. It is a future-proofing slider, not
a recommendation.

The CPU results are the more interesting half. E-Day scales visibly with core
count and with 3D V-Cache, which points at a lot of work on the game thread —
and on a UE5 title the usual suspects are shader compilation and streaming.
We have been here before: [Silent Hill: Townfall cleared 60fps at native 4K on
a 5090 and still
hitched](/blog/silent-hill-townfall-pc-stutter-shader-cache/), because average
frame rate and frame delivery are different problems. Nothing in the benchmarks
I read tells me which one E-Day has. Ask again on the 6th.

I could not confirm two things and would rather say so than guess: GameSpot
published a review without a number I could verify, and a claim circulating
about Series S memory paging traces back to secondhand write-ups of a Digital
Foundry analysis I could not read directly.

## The number

**8**, from five scores between 6 and 9.5, collected on 1 October 2026 and
converted to a ten-point scale.

The mean is doing less work than usual here. A game that one outlet puts at
9.5 and another at 6, with both of them praising the same combat system, is a
game where the thing you care about decides your score. If what you want is
the Gears firefight, every review in the list says it is there. If you want a
campaign that holds together for its full length — the longest in the series,
which The Coalition has been advertising — the verdicts stop agreeing.
