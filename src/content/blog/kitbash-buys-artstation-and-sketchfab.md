Epic Games has sold **ArtStation** and **Sketchfab** to **KitBash** — the company
behind KitBash3D and Greyscalegorilla. Announced this week, **financial terms
undisclosed**.

If your portfolio lives on ArtStation, that is your shop window changing hands.
So let me lay out what is actually known, and what I would keep an eye on.

## What is not changing

More than you would expect, at least on paper:

- Portfolios and libraries stay as they are
- Pricing and subscriptions are unchanged for now
- **Your Epic Games login still works** on both platforms
- KitBash3D and Greyscalegorilla carry on as before

Nobody has to do anything today. Nothing to migrate, nothing to export in a
panic.

## Why Epic let them go

This is Epic tightening up.

Epic bought both in 2021, at the height of the Fortnite money and the metaverse
push, and spent well beyond what was coming in. From 2023 it has been walking
that back — layoffs, and selling things that were not core. Bandcamp went the
same way.

The official line is that Epic is concentrating on **Unreal Engine 6, Fortnite,
the developer ecosystem and the Epic Games Store**. Given [what UE6 is going to
take](/blog/unreal-engine-6-dont-panic/), that is not spin. Merging two engines
and moving the gameplay layer to a new language is enormous. Running an artist
social network is a side quest by comparison.

KitBash's pitch is that this makes one connected ecosystem: learn a technique,
buy or build the asset, publish the work, get discovered, get hired. On the face
of it that is a better fit than sitting inside a games company that mostly
wanted the audience.

## The part worth watching

Here is the tension nobody is saying out loud.

In December 2022, ArtStation was the site where artists **staged the biggest
protest against AI image generation** the industry has seen. Thousands of people
replaced their portfolios with the same red "NO TO AI GENERATED IMAGES" placard
until it took over the trending page. Epic ended up amending its AI FAQ and
adding a **NoAI tag**.

That platform now belongs to a company whose leadership has been openly
enthusiastic about putting AI through its operations.

I want to be careful here, because that is a question, not an accusation. Being
pro-AI is not the same as planning to flood a portfolio site with generated
work — and [I use these tools myself](/blog/ai-in-a-3d-pipeline/), so
I am hardly in a position to be sniffy about it. But the new owner's instincts
and the community's stated position are not obviously pointing the same way, and
how that gets resolved matters more than any feature on a roadmap.

The related complaint already on the table: the ArtStation marketplace has been
filling up with low-effort generated content, and artists have been asking for
tighter quality control for a while. New ownership is the moment that either
gets addressed or gets worse.

Sketchfab is the quieter question. Its marketplace was wound down under Epic and
it is unclear whether it returns, gets folded into KitBash3D, or stays a viewer.
The technical wishlist — gaussian splatting support, mainly — is unanswered.

## What I would actually do

**Nothing urgent.** Genuinely. The continuity promises are specific enough to
take at face value for now.

**But keep your portfolio somewhere you own.** This is the third owner some of
these artists have been through, and the lesson repeats: a platform is a
distribution channel, not a home. Your own domain, your own pages, everything
else pointing at it. That predates this deal — it was true last week
too. It is just that weeks like this one are when people remember it.

**Check your NoAI tag** if you set one in 2022. Settings survive acquisitions
until they do not, and it costs thirty seconds to confirm.

Then get back to work. Ownership changed; the job did not.
