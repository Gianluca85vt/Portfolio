The embargo lifted this morning, 18 November, a day before Grand Theft Auto 6
reaches PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. Across the seven scored verdicts I could
verify by midday, the average is **10**, and the range is nothing at all.
GameSpot, GamesRadar+, TechRadar, Video Games Chronicle, Creative Bloq, Screen
Rant and Inven Global all filed a perfect score.

Treat that figure for exactly what it is: an early snapshot of seven outlets,
taken hours after the gates opened. Neither Metacritic nor OpenCritic had an
aggregate live when I checked, and a hundred more reviews are still landing. A
Rockstar first wave skews high by construction — the outlets with code weeks
early are the ones who could finish an eighty-hour game before launch, and
several say outright that they played well past a hundred hours. The number
that matters arrives in about a week.

What the seven agree on is more useful than the score.

## The 30fps question, answered

In August this blog spent a piece asking [what frame rate the Extended Look
footage was actually running at](/blog/gta-6-extended-look-30fps-question/),
because the trailer gave no honest way to tell. The reviews settle it.
Thirty, locked, on PS5 and PS5 Pro alike. No performance mode, no uncapped
option, no 40fps compromise on a 120Hz panel.

More interesting is how it holds. GamesRadar+ reviewed on a PS5 Pro and
reports no frame-rate fluctuation at all. Creative Bloq calls the lock stable,
with little slowdown or stutter, and says handling and controls feel right
underneath it. That is the hard part. A 30fps target is easy to hit on a
spreadsheet and brutal to hold in a city where the player can turn a corner
into a different density of world. Flat frame times across a world of this
size mean somebody set a streaming budget in year two and refused to let the
art team spend past it, for four more years.

Pop-in does show up. GamesRadar+ saw it only in sessions that ran beyond eight
hours, which is a strange and specific failure. Draw-distance problems do not
wait eight hours; they appear the first time you drive east at sunset. A fault
that needs hours of continuous play to surface looks more like a cache or an
allocator slowly losing the argument with fragmentation. I cannot prove that
from the outside, and nobody who can is talking.

## Where 210 gigabytes went

The install size made sense before the reviews and makes more sense after
them. When the [210GB figure first appeared, the arithmetic pointed at
uncompressed audio and duplicated streaming
data](/blog/gta-6-210gb-where-the-bytes-go/) — the price of never making the
player wait. Reviewers are now describing the thing that budget bought:
interiors you can walk into without a load, pedestrian routines that do not
repeat across a district, weather that arrives rather than switches.

GameSpot's headline says Rockstar set the bar for open worlds impossibly high,
and their review carries three bylines — Ben Janca, Tamoor Hussain and Jake
Dekker. Three people on one review is its own measurement. You do not split a
review three ways unless one person could not see enough of the game in the
time available.

## The awkward part of the week

Two days before any of this, The Game Awards announced its nominees and GTA 6
led with eight, Game of the Year among them, with not one published review in
existence. The jury of a hundred-odd outlets had hands-on time; the public
had a trailer and a release date. The nominations turned out fine. They were
still a vote cast on footage.

Manni L. Perez was confirmed as Lucia Caminos by that nomination list rather
than by Rockstar, which is a small, funny collapse of a marketing plan that
had held the cast secret for three years.

One thing I could not pin down: the exact embargo time. Reporting pointed at
3am on 18 November, and Rockstar never announced anything. The reviews went up
when they went up.
