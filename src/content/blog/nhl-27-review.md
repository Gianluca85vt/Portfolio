The way most people found out that NHL 27 ships generative AI voice was a
podcast. John Buccigross, the ESPN broadcaster who is one half of the game's new
commentary booth, mentioned it on the Chirping Zebras Podcast as though it were
common knowledge — EA had played him lines in his own voice that he had never
recorded, and he thought they sounded pretty good. His summary of the
arrangement has been quoted everywhere since: if it means less work and the same
cheque, he will take it.

EA confirmed it afterwards. The company says it used AI voice technology to
expand recordings from Buccigross and Darren Pang, with their full consent and
active collaboration. Nobody disputes the consent. What nobody announced was the
feature.

## The numbers, and how far apart they are

NHL 27 came out on 11 September on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S — no PC version, full
price, with the Deluxe edition opening seven days early on 4 September.

I can find seven scored verdicts. Analog Stick Gaming gave it 8. The Games
Machine gave it 7.9. Athlon Sports and HackInformer both gave it 7. Game
Informer gave it 6.5. Shacknews gave it 6. DualShockers gave it 5, calling it a
shell of what the series was a decade ago. The mean is 6.77, which rounds to
**7** here.

Read the spread rather than the mean. Three points between the top and bottom
verdict is enormous for an annual sports release, where the usual shape is a
tight cluster half a point wide because everyone is grading the same small delta
against last year. IGN filed without a number I could confirm, and described the
series as stagnating.

My seven skew high against the wider sample. OpenCritic has it at 64 across
thirteen critics, rated Weak, in the bottom quarter of everything they track,
with 31% of critics recommending it. Metacritic sits somewhere near 68. So treat
the 7 as the generous end of the room, and the 31% as the honest middle of it.

## Presentation was the pitch

The August deep dive sold this year almost entirely on presentation: a graphics package rebuilt to match how hockey
looks on television now, team-authentic goal songs, thirty-two team-specific
playbooks where previous years shared three global strategies between every
club, and Connected Franchise back after a long absence. That is a real list. A
lot of it is craft work that fans had asked for.

Reviewers went after the presentation overhaul. Several of the more positive
verdicts single out the on-ice gameplay as improved, so the skating and the
physics come out of this fine. The complaints land on the layer that was
supposed to be the headline, and inside that layer they land hardest on the
audio.

This is the part worth sitting with if you build anything that has to read as
broadcast. A television presentation works as a timing system, and you cannot
upgrade it one asset at a time. The lower-thirds, the replay wipes, the crowd
bed, the way a commentator's pitch rises a quarter-second before the puck
crosses the line — those things carry the illusion together, and the audio does
more of the lifting than the graphics do. You can rebuild every overlay in the
package and still have the whole thing read as a video game the moment the voice
over the top of it arrives flat and late.

Reviewers described the Buccigross and Pang booth as low-energy and thin. Some
of that is a first year for any new commentary pairing, which is always rough —
the chemistry is recorded separately and assembled, and two broadcasters who
sound great calling a live game can sound like strangers when the lines are cut
apart and reassembled by a game engine. But a synthesised line has a specific
failure mode on top of that, and Buccigross himself gave the clearest example of
it: the model said *chairs* where a hockey broadcaster would say *seats*.

That is the whole problem in one word. The generated audio is technically clean.
It scans as his voice. And it gets the vernacular wrong in a way no hockey fan
will miss and no spectrogram will catch. Anyone who has shipped a generated
texture or a retargeted animation knows this shape — the asset is ninety-five
per cent right, and the five per cent is precisely the part the audience is
expert in.

## Two annual sports games, one month

The comparison sitting right there is NBA 2K27, which this blog looked at on 2
September and scored 8.5 off a much thinner sample. The praise there went to
[defence built from footage broadcast cameras barely show](/blog/nba-2k27-review/)
— a team that went and solved a presentation problem the hard way, by finding
reference nobody had bothered to shoot properly. Same genre, same annual
treadmill, same broadcast ambition. One of them spent the year on the part
nobody could fake.

There is also a licensing angle that is starting to look prescient. When
Saber's Space Marine 3 confirmed last week that
[Games Workshop's licence forbids generative AI outright](/blog/space-marine-3-no-generative-ai-games-workshop/),
it read as a rights-holder being cautious. A week later, the loudest complaint
about a full-price sports sim is a voice track that was expanded with exactly
that technology, disclosed by accident. The NHL and NHLPA licences evidently
permit it. Whether the next round of negotiations still does is a different
question.

## Where that leaves it

A 7 off seven verdicts, on a range from 5 to 8, describing a game that plays
better than it presents in a year sold on presentation. If you play hockey games
you have made this calculation before and you will make it again next September.

The genAI disclosure is the part that will outlast the score. The voice lines
themselves are hardly a scandal — the broadcasters agreed, got paid, and one of
them is openly pleased about the reduced studio time. What sticks is that a
full-price annual release shipped a synthesised performance inside its headline
feature without saying so, and the first person to mention it was the man whose
voice it was.
