ABEMA named the three winners of its first Project PRISMation open call on
**6 November**. The number that explains the shape of the whole scheme is
2.13 million — what the project's first pilot, *Poppin-Play Kitchen*, had done
on YouTube by 14 May, on ABEMA's own count. Four and a half minutes of
animation, made by a creator working out of Thailand under the name Zemyata. It
was greenlit for a sequel in May off the back of that counter. That is the
model.

So the call that closed on 31 August was asking for premises that could survive
being four minutes long in public. It is a narrow brief.

## Who got picked

Three works, all dated 2027 or later.

- **OXY-HEAVEN**, by Waboku — a steampunk ensemble drama set in a café run by a
  former doctor, on a ship-nation in postwar reconstruction.
- **Amneka**, by a creator working as orange — a fantasy comedy at a small
  school, where one girl's secret starts leaking into everybody else's day.
- **Kandy-Killers for Hire**, by Dominique Garrow, who also goes by koilccc, a
  US-based freelance illustrator and animator. The Japanese coverage files it
  as psycho-pop: an assassination startup heading for bankruptcy.

Comic Natalie put Waboku in its headline and left the other two names out,
which tells you which of the three the Japanese trade press already had a file
on. The call itself carried no professional requirement and no condition on age,
nationality or gender, and ran from May to 31 August.

## What four minutes costs the person making it

A trailer can be cut from the best twenty seconds of anything. A pilot has to
hold a scene, which means character designs that survive being turned, layout,
in-betweens, colour, compositing, a sound mix, voices that match mouths. Every
department of a television series, at a hundredth of the length, usually resting
on one or two people. No department to hand it to. Four minutes of finished animation is months of evenings.

Which is why the support list ABEMA published repays reading. The platform says it handles development, voice casting, music,
translation and promotion. Read that as an inventory of what a solo animator
cannot buy: a casting director, a composer, a localisation pass, and an
audience. The drawing is the part they already have.

## Why a platform commissions the pilot instead of the series

A twelve-episode late-night show is a production committee, two years and a lot
of other people's money spent before anyone outside the room finds out whether
it works. Commissioning four minutes turns that around. Fund the short, publish
it free, watch what happens, decide afterwards. ABEMA ran exactly that sequence
once already — pilot in January, sequel in May.

It is the same instinct as putting the money and the novelty at the front of
the pipeline, which is where [Rakuten Viki's animated K-drama kept its AI
work](/blog/the-painted-soul-viki-ai-pre-production/), well clear of the
animation itself. The concept stage is where being wrong is survivable.

The capacity problem makes it look smarter still. Nobody has spare studio weeks
sitting idle — [Chiikawa stopped making new episodes for thirteen weeks to
rebuild a buffer](/blog/chiikawa-break-production-buffer/), on a show carrying the
year's number one film in Japan. Commissioning a short from
someone outside the studio system competes for none of those weeks.

## What is not public

No budget figures. ABEMA has not said what a PRISMation pilot is funded at, how
long the three have to deliver, or what threshold turns a pilot into a series —
and the view count that preceded the one greenlight we have seen is a result,
not a published rule. I would not read 2.13 million as the bar.

The three announced on Friday will be judged the way the first batch was: free,
in public, by whoever clicks in the first week. That is a cheaper failure than a
committee, and a far more visible one.
