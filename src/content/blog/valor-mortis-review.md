A soulslike asks you to read a wind-up. Which arm, which arc, how many frames
before it lands. Third person hands you the whole enemy in frame, your own body
for scale, and a camera far enough back that an attack begins and ends inside
the picture. Move that camera into the player's head. Most of that goes.

One More Level shipped Valor Mortis into that problem on 13 October. Across every
verdict I can verify — from the 6s to the 9s, from outlets that liked it and
outlets that did not — the parry is the thing singled out as working. That I did
not expect.

## The number

Seven scored verdicts, mean **7.5**, running from 6 to 9. Metacritic read 78 on
18 October and OpenCritic 81, and Metacritic's own range of individual reviews
is wider than my seven — 50 at the floor, 90 at the ceiling.

Three points on a ten-point scale is a genuine disagreement. The shape of it is
familiar, too. [Hellraiser Revival split four points wide](/blog/hellraiser-revival-review/)
ten days ago: unanimous on the look, divided on the fighting. Valor Mortis
inverts which half is settled. The combat is what the 6s and the 9s agree on.

## Where a first-person parry has to put its tells

In third person the silhouette does the work. Onimusha leaned on that so hard
that [animation ends up carrying the entire combat system](/blog/onimusha-way-of-the-sword-review/) —
read the shape, read the punish window, answer. The camera sits back and the
enemy's whole body is the UI.

First person deletes three of those affordances at once. No player body, so no
reference for reach. A narrower field of view, so a wide swing starts outside
the frame. And an enemy close enough that their torso fills the screen while
the arm doing the damage is somewhere past the edge of it.

What is left is a short list. The upper body, held a beat
longer than it would be in third person. Audio — a scrape, an intake of breath,
a step that lands on a different surface. Weapon flash. And the one thing first
person gives you for free, which is that the enemy's eyeline points at you
rather than at your avatar.

That the parry is the consensus highlight across a three-point spread tells me
those frames got the attention. Vincent Cassel voicing Napoleon will get the
headlines. The anticipation poses are the expensive part.

## Where the points came off

GamesRadar's Abbie Stone went to 6 on 12 October, calling the ideas more
familiar than her own shadow. GameSpot's 6 faults a slow opening and a small
enemy roster. Game Informer's 7 says it falls short of its own premise while
being a confident step into the genre. Push Square's verdict is that it starts
stronger than it finishes, with boss platforming it has no patience for. Round
up the criticism across the roundups and you get bugs, uneven difficulty and a
bestiary that runs out.

The platforming complaint is the one I find most legible from the outside. One
More Level came from Ghostrunner, where traversal was momentum and instant
restarts and death cost you two seconds. Soulslike pacing fights that on
principle — stamina, lock-on, a run back to the boss. Opposite instincts. Bolt a scripted jump
sequence to it and you are asking a player to judge a gap with no feet in
frame, in a game that has spent ten hours teaching them to stand still and
watch an elbow.

It is €39.99, it is on PS5, Xbox Series and PC, and it went day one on Game
Pass — which is the detail that makes the number softer than it looks. Lyrical
Games picked this up after Private Division let it go, and a 7.5 you already
have access to is a different proposition from a 7.5 at the counter.
