Two numbers, four days old. *Avengers: Endgame* sits at $2.9255 billion worldwide. *Avatar* sits at $2.9237 billion. That is a gap of about $1.8 million on a total near three billion — six hundredths of one per cent, which is well inside the wobble on international weekend estimates. The chart changed hands on 4 October 2026. It could change hands again when the actuals come in.

The thing that moved it is *Avengers Endgame: Encore*, which went back into cinemas on 25 September 2026 and had taken roughly $126 million globally by the time the record flipped. The run exists to clear a runway for *Avengers: Doomsday* on 18 December.

So the chart is a re-release chart. It has been for years. Endgame first went past Avatar in July 2019, Avatar took it back with a China re-release in 2021, and everything since has been studios deciding when to put a finished film back in rooms that sell $25 tickets. Nominal dollars, no inflation adjustment, no attendance figure anywhere in it.

## Four minutes is not a small ask

What makes Encore worth looking at from a pipeline seat is that it is not a reprint. Marvel added about four minutes of new footage — an extended version of the final scene, and three post-credits stingers where the 2019 cut had none. Reporting says the new material was shot during *Doomsday* production, which is the sane way to do it: the crew, the stages and the lighting are already standing.

Then it has to go into a film from 2019.

That is the part people underrate. A delivered master is a flattened thing. To put a new performance into the Steve-and-Peggy dance you do not touch the master — you go back to the plate, the roto, the precomps, the camera data, the project file that knew where the lights were. Seven years of that, sitting on whatever Marvel's vendors were archiving to in 2019, in tool versions that have since moved several releases. Anyone who has tried to reopen a four-year-old scene and found the textures relinked to a server that no longer exists knows the shape of this. We have written before about [LTO-10 drives that cannot read an LTO-9 tape](/blog/lto-10-cannot-read-lto-9-tapes/). A tape you cannot mount is at least a known quantity. A project file whose plugins no longer build is a worse morning.

The grade is the other tell. Existing sequences in Encore were regraded. That means a re-conform: the edit rebuilt against original sources, pushed back through a colour pipeline that in 2026 is not the pipeline that signed off the 2019 DI, on reference displays that are not the 2019 displays. Studios do this because the alternative looks worse. Cut 2026 footage straight into a 2019 master and the join shows — in the blacks first, usually, then in skin.

## The ending that two rooms get differently

The new *Doomsday* stinger is restricted to IMAX and Infinity Vision-certified screens. Which means the film now ends in two places depending on which auditorium you bought into.

Infinity Vision came up here [yesterday, as a room specification rather than a camera format](/blog/avengers-doomsday-imax-infinity-vision/) — a certification covering the projector, the screen and the sound, which cinemas qualify for. Encore uses that certification to gate story content rather than image quality, which is a new job for it. A format exclusive used to mean a taller frame. Here it means a scene.

That lands on the edit. The cut has to work as a complete film in the room that does not get the scene, which rules out building toward it. Marketing gets a reason to upsell a ticket; editorial gets two endings to balance.

## What is still unknown

Marvel has not said which vendors did the new compositing work, and nobody has published a shot count for the regrade. The Russo brothers have talked about why they went back to the ending; the pipeline side of it has not been detailed anywhere I can find, and I am not going to guess at it.

What is on the record is the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is thin. A $1.8 million margin means the all-time crown currently belongs to whichever film's international reporting gets trued up last.
