Rockstar put 27 minutes of Grand Theft Auto VI on Netflix at 3pm Eastern, six
hours before the rest of the internet got it. The game is dated **19 November
2026**. The footage was, in Rockstar's wording, captured entirely from in-game
footage on PlayStation 5 — and, per the coverage, a base PS5 rather than a Pro.

Rockstar did not say how many frames a second that was. It has never said.

Which has not stopped "4K 30fps confirmed" from appearing in headlines all
evening. That number is a deduction made by people watching a video, and it is
worth separating from the things Rockstar actually stated, because the
reasoning behind it is more interesting than the number.

## What was in the 27 minutes

Jason and Lucia, in Leonida, working a pickup for someone called Boobie. Raul
turns up as a getaway passenger. There is a flashback to the pair being caught
after a heist that went wrong, which reads like the setup for the whole game.
Phil Collins is in there, playing in an elevator.

The systems list is the part I keep going back to. Both protagonists are live
at once, with the one you are not driving joining in on shooting and robberies.
Cars can be opened with a slim jim instead of a broken window, and burned
afterwards to drop a tail. Cutscenes hand back to gameplay without a seam. There
are e-scooters, gyms, diving, rally racing, fishing, arcade cabinets you can
actually play, clubs you can drink and dance in, and smaller heists between the
big ones. Pet a dog and a little angel icon appears, which means something
somewhere is keeping score of how you behave.

I have not sat with the footage frame by frame. This is written a couple of
hours after the stream, from what has been published and from what Rockstar
itself put out, and the shot-by-shot reads going up elsewhere tonight were
typed just as fast.

## The frame rate nobody confirmed

Digital Foundry has been making the same argument since June, and the Extended
Look has not changed it: a locked 60 on this game is unlikely, on PS5 and on
PS5 Pro alike.

The reason is the part that gets lost. When people argue about performance modes
they are usually arguing about pixels — resolution, shadow quality, ray tracing,
all the things a bigger GPU buys you. GTA is not primarily a GPU problem. It is
a simulation problem. Traffic that routes itself, pedestrians with somewhere to
be, physics on every vehicle in range, police that coordinate, and now a second
protagonist running combat behaviour beside you at all times.

That work sits on the CPU. And the PS5 Pro, whatever else it upgraded, did not
meaningfully upgrade the CPU — it is essentially the same part as the base
machine, clocked a little higher. A bigger GPU renders the same simulation
faster. It does not let the simulation itself tick twice as often.

So a Pro performance mode does not follow automatically from a Pro existing.
That is the whole argument, and it has been the argument for a year.

## What the feature list costs

Take the angel icon. A morality credit for petting a dog means a value being
tracked, updated and persisted for something as small as one interaction with
one animal. On its own that is nothing. As a pattern across a world this size —
every business, every NPC, every optional thing you can do — it is a lot of
state being kept warm.

Same with the two-character system. An AI partner who joins a firefight is not a
cutscene; it is a second agent doing perception, pathing and target selection
every frame, in the same street already full of pedestrians and cars.

Interior density is the other one. The clubs and diners people are calling
ridiculous are ridiculous in a specific way: they are full of individually
simulated people in a space small enough that you see all of them at once.
Outdoors you can thin a crowd with distance. In a room you cannot.

All of it is a budget, and 30 is where the budget lands. Rockstar has spent a
decade being the studio that picks simulation over frame rate, and this is what
that choice looks like when it ships.

## The honest state of it

Confirmed: 19 November 2026, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, 27 minutes, captured
in-game on a base PS5.

Not confirmed: frame rate, resolution, performance modes, whether a 60fps mode
turns up later on Pro hardware or on PC.
