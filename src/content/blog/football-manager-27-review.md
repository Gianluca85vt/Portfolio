The headline change in Football Manager 27 is that the dots got smaller.

Sports Interactive's own feature post on the 2D match view says the ball and
the player discs were shrunk so they sit more believably on the pitch, with the
shadows redone and the net made less opaque. Months of somebody's year,
described in a paragraph. It is also the most honest summary of this release
available, because last year the dots were too big and the studio appears to
have found that out from its forums.

Verdicts started landing on 7 November, two days ahead of the 10 November
release — SEGA held reviews to the day before and some outlets went a little
early. Four of them carry a number I could convert and check. The mean is
**8.0**, running from 7.5 to 8.9. Metacritic was showing 82 from seven critics
on 8 November, so this is a thin field and an early average, and it will move.

A 1.4-point spread on a management game is normal. The four reviewers are
measuring against different things, though, and one of them says so out loud.
Cubed3 put the figure at 8 against Football
Manager 26 and no higher than 5 against the entries before it, because the
day-to-day navigation is still a maze. That is the most useful sentence written
about FM27 so far.

## What FM26 cost

Football Manager 26 holds the lowest Metacritic rating in the series — 68 from
44 critics — and its Steam user reviews settled at Mostly Negative, around 22
per cent positive. That is interface damage. Nobody walks away from a
management game over defensive line height. They go because the screen they
have to look at four hundred times a season got worse than the one it replaced,
and FM26's interface, rebuilt for the series' move to Unity, was that screen.

The engine move gets reported loosely, so: Unity was a presentation and
platform change. The simulation underneath stayed on Sports Interactive's own
systems, where it has always lived. FM26 broke the rewritten part and kept the
rest. Textbook.

FM27's answer is a rebuild of that interface — fewer clicks, messages you can
act on from where you read them, more customisation. The Games Machine calls
the result clearly improved and squarely PC-first again, with a general lack of
polish and a thin set of official licences as the remaining faults. Forbes, in
a first look in late October, said the poor interface decisions had been
rectified. There was also a demo. Gamesplanet's store notes say a free one ran
on Steam until 3 November, a week before release, which is a studio working out
that it has to show rather than say.

## The engine is still arguing

Multiplayer.it, the lowest of the four, puts the tactical system down as the
most convincing part of the game and defending as the weakest thing in the
match engine. Cubed3 flags goalkeeper animation glitches and too many
low-scoring matches. Posters on Sports Interactive's own feedback board say the
engine over-rewards width and underuses central midfield. Forum sentiment is
forum sentiment. Still, three independent complaints all pointing at the
defensive third reads as a pattern.

That split — a tactical layer people trust, sitting under a presentation layer
that keeps undermining it — is a shape worth recognising. It turned up in
[a tactics game where the plan reads fine and the camera does
not](/blog/star-wars-zero-company-review-tactics-camera/), and it turned up in
[a football game whose year of work went somewhere other than the
football](/blog/ea-sports-fc-27-review/). The underlying system can be right
while the thing you look at makes you distrust it. For a simulation that has
nothing to show you but a display, that is not a cosmetic problem.

## What the score is measuring

Eight out of ten, against a predecessor that scored 68. Reviewers who have
been playing this series for fifteen years are grading a recovery here, and a
recovery grades generously. Digital Citizen,
positive throughout, still lists the season review and average player position
stats as missing — features that existed in earlier entries and have not come
back.

Read plainly, 8.0 says FM27 is the game FM26 should have been, a year late,
and that the series spent two development cycles getting back to a line it had
already crossed. Credit where it is due: the interface team did the work. It
still cost two years.

If you skipped FM26, this is where to come back. If you played it, you already
know what was broken, and four reviewers are telling you it is not broken any
more.
