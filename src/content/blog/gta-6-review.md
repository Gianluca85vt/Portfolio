Grand Theft Auto VI launches tomorrow, 19 November, on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S,
and the review embargo lifted today. I count sixteen scored verdicts I could
read and verify this afternoon. They average **9.8**, which the score box rounds
to a 10, and none of them drops below a 9. Ten of the sixteen are perfect
scores.

The aggregators say the same thing louder. Metacritic has the PS5 version at
99 from 100 critic reviews, every one of them positive, and the Xbox Series X
version at 99 from nine. OpenCritic showed a Top Critic Average of 98 from 50
reviews with 100 percent recommending. Both counts as of this afternoon, and
both still climbing.

A one-point spread across sixteen outlets is rare for anything, and it changes what is worth reading in these reviews. When everyone
agrees on the score, the useful information sits in the few sentences where a
reviewer stops praising.

## What the reviews agree on

The city first. VGC calls Vice City the most remarkable open world ever built,
and that line, in one wording or another, is in almost every verdict on the
board. Edge, The Guardian, Giant Bomb and Digital Spy all gave it full marks.
GRYOnline.pl and Worth Playing landed at 9.5 and still read like reviews of the
best game either has covered.

Then the two leads. Jason and Lucia's relationship is where the story reviews
spend their warmest paragraphs, and VGC ranks their story as Rockstar's best. The main story runs about 60 hours before side content, by VGC's count.
Inven Global put in more than 80 and reached roughly 75 hours for a full
completion; Wccftech filed after 90.

## Where they stop praising

There are three complaints in the reviews I could read, and one of them shows
up far more than the others.

**The frame rate.** Rockstar confirmed on 10 November that the game targets
30fps on every console, PS5 Pro included, with the Pro spending its extra power
on PSSR upscaling and ray-traced shadows instead of a performance mode. The
reviews add the part a spec sheet leaves out. VGC's verdict notes occasional
frame-rate drops in the densest parts of the city, while stressing they were
rare. The Outerhaven names the missing 60fps mode as a flaw outright. Inven
Global, on a base PS5, found it smooth.

That is the outcome this blog argued for in August, when
[the 30fps question was still a deduction from the Extended Look](/blog/gta-6-extended-look-30fps-question/):
the limit on a city this busy is the CPU running the simulation, and a bigger
GPU does not make the simulation tick twice as often. Rockstar has since piled
more onto that same processor, with
[weather that is local and keeps its state](/blog/gta-6-weather-localized-persistent/)
across the map. The Pro getting shadows instead of frames is the same argument
again: the extra hardware went to the GPU, so it bought light.

**The opening.** Inven Global, the most specific of the critical voices, found
the first chapter slow and noted that a lot of the side content stays locked if
you push off the main story early. A Rockstar opening that holds the sandbox back
until the story has introduced everyone is not new, and at 60 hours the
patience pays back, but it is the first thing a lot of players will meet.

**Small technical rough edges.** The Outerhaven mentions a few technical
imperfections without making much of them, and PPE.pl talks about a handful of
clear shortcomings while still giving it a 10. Nobody I read describes anything
that breaks a mission.

## What the numbers leave out

Every score above comes from a review build played before launch, and none of
it covers a PC version, because Rockstar has not announced one.

The other thing the scores cannot carry is how 30fps feels to the person
holding the pad. For most of these reviewers it clearly did not matter much
next to everything else on screen. For a player coming from a year of 60fps
action games it will be the first thing they notice and, after an hour in Vice
City, probably not the thing they remember.
