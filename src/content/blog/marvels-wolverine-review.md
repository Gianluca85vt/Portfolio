The embargo went up at 8am Pacific on 10 September 2026, five days ahead of the
15 September PS5 launch. Publishers hand out that much runway when they expect
to like what comes back.

What came back was a five-point spread.

Across the twenty-four scored verdicts I could verify on the day, the average
is **7.5**. The range runs from a 5 at Giant Bomb to a pair of 10s at ScreenHub
and Hardcore Gamer. Metacritic sits at 78; OpenCritic at 80. Both aggregates
are drawn from roughly a hundred reviews, and Metacritic counts none of them as
negative — on its scale a 50 lands in "mixed", which is where the floor of this
distribution sits.

Read the middle of that and you get a good game. Read the edges and you get two
different games.

## Where the split falls

Look at how uniform the low cluster is. IGN,
GameSpot, GamesRadar+, Eurogamer, VGC, The Guardian, Gamekult and ComicBook all
filed a 60. Eight of the largest English-language outlets, and one French one,
arriving at the same number independently. Giant Bomb went a notch under.

The high tail is a longer list of smaller sites, and it is genuinely enthusiastic
rather than generous — 100s, 90s, and something close to thirty reviews at 80.

Those two groups are not disagreeing about what the game does. Read the copy and
the praise is nearly identical on both sides: combat, gore, visuals, performance
capture. The complaints are identical too — the story, and how often the game
repeats itself. What differs is the weight. If you grade a Wolverine game on
whether the fighting feels like being Wolverine, this is a 9. If you grade it on
whether the writing earns its R rating, it is a 6.

Metacritic has this as the lowest-scored game from a PlayStation Studios team in
the six years of the PS5. The Spider-Man games Insomniac made before it landed
at 87, 85 and 90. That comparison is doing a lot of work in the coverage today,
and it sets a bar almost nothing clears.

## What everybody agreed on is the expensive part

The unanimous praise is for the things that cost money in a pipeline, and I
think that is worth saying out loud, because those are the things a studio can
buy.

Take the gore, which is the game's whole proposition. A body that comes apart
along claw lines is not a texture effect. It needs cut states authored per
enemy, wound geometry that closes correctly wherever the blade passed, decals
that respect the surface underneath, and severed pieces that are real meshes
with real weight. Multiply that by every enemy archetype and every angle a
player can attack from. Then run the same system backwards on Logan himself,
because the healing factor means his damage state has to resolve in view of the
camera, continuously, while he is still fighting. Damage that only accumulates
is a solved problem. Damage that has to reverse on the hero model, on screen, is
a much longer conversation with the character team.

The environments got the same unanimous nod, which is the return on a trip this
blog wrote about last week: [six people, four regions and 189,589 photographs of
Japan](/blog/insomniac-wolverine-japan-location-scanning). Location scanning is
one of the few line items where you can draw a fairly straight line from spend
to reception. Nobody in the low cluster docked a point for how the world looks.

Performance held too, which matters because Insomniac shipped something unusual
here — [ray tracing at 60fps on a base PS5](/blog/marvels-wolverine-ray-tracing-60fps-base-ps5),
not the Pro, as the default rather than a mode you opt into. Reviewers on both
ends of the spread ran it without complaint. On a launch this size that is not
nothing.

None of that budget reaches the script. A scanning trip cannot fix a second act,
and a dismemberment system cannot make a mission structure feel less repeated.
The split in the scores is a split between the parts of a game money can buy and
the part it cannot.

## The number

**7.5**, from twenty-four verdicts between 5 and 10, collected on 10 September
2026 and converted to a ten-point scale.

Treat it more loosely than usual. A mean is a reasonable summary when the
verdicts cluster — the Onimusha reviews two weeks ago ran from 7 to 9.2 and
their 8.5 told you something real about [what a tight spread means](/blog/onimusha-way-of-the-sword-review).
Here, half the sample sits at 6 and the other half at 8 or above, and 7.5 is a
value almost nobody gave it. The spread is the more useful figure. If
what you want from a Wolverine game is the fighting, weight the top half. If you
want it to be Spider-Man 2 with claws, the eight 60s are telling you something.
