Aniplex and TYPE-MOON put out a trailer on 22 November for the premium-format
run of ufotable's *Witch on the Holy Night*, and buried in it is a number worth
stopping on. The ScreenX version carries **roughly 981 cuts** of side-wall
footage, covering 67 minutes of a film that runs 129. Those screenings start in
Japan on 27 November, a week after the film opened on the 20th, alongside Ultra
4DX, Dolby Cinema, 4DX and MX4D.

First, what the 981 is not. It is not 981 new scenes in the film, and it is not
an extended cut. It counts the cuts for which something new was made to fill the
two auditorium walls that ScreenX projects onto. Anyone who sees the headline
and books a ticket expecting more story is going to be disappointed by a
beautiful corridor.

## Why this format is expensive for drawings

ScreenX is a CJ 4DPLEX idea: the main screen plus the left and right walls of
the room, pushed out to about 270 degrees of coverage. For live action it is a
post problem with a known solution. The negative is wider than the frame that
got used, there is usually second-unit material lying around, and a plate can be
extended with the same tools that remove a boom mic. Something exists outside
the crop.

Drawn animation has no outside. A background painting is made to the layout, and
the layout was drawn to the frame — a little margin for the camera to move
inside, and then it stops, because paint costs money and nobody was ever going
to see the edge. Widening a cut therefore means painting more of a place that was
never designed past its own border. Where the original was flat art with a pan
across it, that is a background artist doing new work, in the original's style,
two years after the rest of the department moved on.

Doing it 981 times is a production of its own.

## What ufotable had already built

The studio is unusually well placed for this, for a reason that has nothing to do
with ScreenX. ufotable's signature camera moves are drawn layers riding 3D
geometry — a real set exists in the scene file, the characters are cels on it,
and the push through a room holds its perspective because something
three-dimensional is carrying it. When a cut like that needs to be wider, the
geometry is already there. You move the frustum and render again.

That only solves the environment. The character layers stay where they were,
since nobody animated a drawing for a part of the room the camera was not
pointing at, so the walls will be set and atmosphere while the people remain on
the screen in front of you. Which is the correct call, and also the reason a
ScreenX anime tends to feel like sitting inside the background rather than
inside the shot.

The other half of the studio's advantage is the department I wrote about when the
film's staff list landed: everything distinctive here happens after the drawing
is finished, in [the compositing pass ufotable's photography department
runs](/blog/witch-on-the-holy-night-ufotable-photography/). A studio that already
treats light as a separate authored layer can grade a side panel differently from
the main screen. One that bakes its look into the background paint cannot.

## The 67 minutes are the interesting part

Half the film has walls. Half does not.

*Mahoyo* is a TYPE-MOON visual novel, and visual novels are two people in a room
talking for long stretches. When I looked at the final trailer in October the
thing that stood out was how much of the running time was likely to be interiors
with a conversation in them — gorgeous ones, lit to within an inch of their
lives, but rooms. There is nothing to put on a side wall during a close-up. Any
honest answer is to leave those panels dark or hold a soft ambient field, and 62
minutes of this film is where that answer lives.

So the 981 cuts are concentrated in the other half: the night exteriors, the
magic, the snow, the fights. That is a sensible distribution and it is also an
admission about what the format is good for.

## The projection problem nobody puts in a press release

A premium format is a specification for a room, not a property of the film — I
made that argument when Marvel pulled *Avengers: Doomsday* out of IMAX and
[Infinity Vision turned out to be a room spec](/blog/avengers-doomsday-imax-infinity-vision/),
and it applies harder here. ScreenX walls are walls. They are not screen
material, they are lit at a different throw distance and an acute angle, and they
have no meaningful black level because you are projecting onto architecture.

Take a comp built on diffusion, bloom and glowing light sources, which is exactly
what a ufotable night scene is, and put it on that surface. The glow spreads. The
contrast that was doing the work collapses toward grey. A wall panel needs its own
grade — darker, harder, with the bloom pulled back — or the room reads as a haze
with a sharp rectangle in the middle of it.

Whether they did that is the thing I would want to know, and it is not something
a trailer shown on YouTube can tell you, because the trailer is a 16:9 video of a
format that only exists in a specific building. The international run starts
21 January 2027, with the United States and Canada on the 29th, and I have not
seen a word yet about which of those dates carries the ScreenX version. For
anyone outside Japan, 981 repainted cuts currently exist as a number in an
announcement.
