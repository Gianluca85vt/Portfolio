### Watching as a giant star swallowed its neighbor and exploded
Fri, 20 Nov 2026 20:41:17 +0000 — https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/11/astronomers-watched-a-star-spiral-to-its-death-inside-a-giant-companion/

In the Andromeda Galaxy, a giant star about seven times as massive as the Sun spent nearly a decade slowly swallowing its much smaller companion. The process, known as common envelope evolution, was proposed in the 1970s to explain how some of the tightest pairs of stars in the Universe form, but no one had ever caught it in the act. In a new Nature study, astronomers led by Viraj Karambelkar, an astrophysicist at Columbia University, finally witnessed the entire spectacle from the decades before it began to the eruption that ended it in late 2025. “It lasts for a few years, maybe a few decades,” Karambelkar said. “Stars evolve on time scales of millions of years. So, it's really like a blip on the entire cosmic time scale.” Shared envelopes Some pairs of stars orbit so close to each other that they could not have formed at that distance, so their orbits must have shrunk. Common envelope evolution is the leading explanation for that shrinkage. When one star in a binary grows old, it expands into a giant, and its outer layers can engulf its companion. The two then share a single, loosely bound envelope of gas. As the engulfed star drags through it, it loses orbital energy to friction and spirals inward.
