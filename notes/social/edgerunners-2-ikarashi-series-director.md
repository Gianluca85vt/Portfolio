Studio Trigger handed a ten-episode series to the man who directed one episode of the last one. That is the decision worth talking about in Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2, which lands on Netflix with all ten episodes at once on 20 October.

Kai Ikarashi is directing his first series. He got the job off the sixth episode of the 2022 season, "Girl on Fire," and anyone who has worked on a series knows why that is both the right call and a risk.

An episode is where a studio concentrates. One director, one animation director, a storyboard somebody has lived with for months, and a slot in the schedule the other episodes are quietly subsidising. The episode people remember as the one that looked incredible is very often the episode that got the key animators — and you can usually find the two either side of it that paid for it.

Running the series is a different job. You stop achieving a look and start specifying one: model sheets, colour scripts, rules for what the line does when the camera moves, all of it executed by people you did not hand-pick, in weeks you are not in the room for. Plenty of excellent episode directors never make that transition work.

Two other production details I like here. Lead character designer Ichigo Kanno has said the sequel takes a different aesthetic from season one, and showrunner Bartosz Sztybor has framed the tone around 90s cinema. A sequel with the same cast in the same city is the cheap sequel — the model sheets, colour keys and background plates are already on the server. New cast plus a declared design change throws that library away deliberately. They had the cheap option and did not take it.

And the batch release is a schedule decision before it is a marketing one. Ten episodes delivered together means every one is finished before the first streams, which removes the failure mode that eats most television animation.
