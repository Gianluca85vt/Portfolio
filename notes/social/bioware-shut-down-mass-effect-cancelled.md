## LinkedIn

EA cut around 400 jobs on 21 October. About fifty of them were at BioWare, and BioWare is the studio that closed.

That ratio is the whole obituary. Thirty-odd years, two of the most valuable RPG names anybody owns, and what was left to clear out at the end was fifty people. The Inquisition credits ran to hundreds. Veilguard still had a real team in 2024. Wednesday's announcement closed a door on a room that had already been emptied twice.

EA was specific about where the investment goes instead: sports, Apex Legends, Battlefield, the next Star Wars Jedi game, Iron Man. Read that as a production manager rather than as a fan. Every item is live-service or a licensed action-adventure with a fixed-length campaign. Nothing on it is a seventy-hour single-player RPG, and the reason is amortisation.

An environment artist building a Battlefield map or an FC stadium builds something that gets loaded tens of thousands of times by one player and millions of times across an audience, for years. The lighting, the decals, the LOD chain — it all comes back. Arlathan Forest does not work like that. A player walks through it for two hours and never returns. Same asset cost. A thousandth of the playtime. That ratio sits on a spreadsheet at every publisher in the business, and it is why the big single-player RPG keeps being the line item that goes, even when the art is the part everyone praises.

Which is what stings about this one. Veilguard's reception split hard on writing and tone, and the environment work came out of that argument intact. The art team is not why BioWare closed.

What disappears now is not the IP. Electronic Arts keeps Mass Effect and Dragon Age. What disappears is thirty years of in-house tooling that only made sense in the building it grew in, and the one person who remembered which nodes in the shader library were load-bearing. There is no handover in a closure. People get an email and a date.

Fifty people. Then none. The Edmonton office closes.
