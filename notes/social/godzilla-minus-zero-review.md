Godzilla Minus Zero opened in Japan today and the aggregate scores are telling you much less than they appear to.

Rotten Tomatoes has run between 92 and 97 percent over the last two days. Metacritic, which weights the actual numbers rather than counting thumbs, sits at 78 from 23 critics. I gathered the seven verdicts I could find that carried a real number, converted everything to a ten-point scale, and got a mean of 7.2 across a spread from 6 to 10. JoBlo at 10. Empire, Time Out and Dread Central all at 3 out of 5.

A four-point spread on a film everyone agrees looks superb is worth sitting with, because the agreement and the disagreement fall on different sides of the pipeline.

The effects are praised in every single review I read, including all three of the harshest ones. Dread Central picked out the sound design of monster movement in the IMAX mix. Several critics say the work looks better than Minus One's, which is a serious claim about a film that won the visual effects Oscar with a crew of roughly thirty-five people at Shirogumi.

The complaints are about pacing, sentimentality, and how little Godzilla is in the Godzilla film.

Those two facts fit together more neatly than the reviews suggest. Takashi Yamazaki shot this one for IMAX, the first Japanese production to do it. A 1.43:1 frame is taller, carries more pixels, and fills the parts of the image a widescreen crop would have thrown away with ground and sky that now have to survive a sixty-foot screen. Per-shot cost goes up. Crew size and schedule do not scale with it.

Fewer monster sequences, each finished to a higher standard, is what that arithmetic produces. Toho has published no shot count and no crew figure for the sequel, so nobody can check me on it yet.

One practical note: it carries an R rating, the first in franchise history.
