GTA 6 ships with over 600,000 NPC animations. Almost eleven times GTA 5, twice
Red Dead Redemption 2 — and the number that matters is how many people it took.

Rockstar North's Aaron Garbut gave those figures to the New York Times back in
August, and they have been resurfacing all week as the game lands. GTA 5 had
about 55,000 clips. Red Dead 2 had roughly 300,000. GTA 6 has over 600,000.

Getting to a big animation count is not hard on its own. A few hundred source
clips, randomised playback, additive noise layered on a base loop, and you can
report an enormous number with a straight face. Most crowds in most games are
built roughly this way, and it is a perfectly sound technique.

Garbut's claim is different. He says Rockstar art-directed each individual one
of them. That means a person looked at the clip, formed an opinion, and either
kept it or sent it back — six hundred thousand times. That is not a rendering
problem. It is a staffing and review problem that somebody had to keep running
for years without the library turning into a folder nobody can search.

Rough arithmetic: Red Dead 2 shipped in October 2018. Six hundred thousand
clips across the 2,946 days to launch is a little over 200 a day, weekends
included. The real curve was nothing like flat, but the order of magnitude is a
building full of animators. Rockstar reportedly stood up a dedicated Los
Angeles team of writers, directors and producers aimed squarely at NPCs.
Writers. For background characters.

Then every one of those clips has to be named, tagged, retargeted across body
types, and fitted into a streaming budget, because 600,000 clips do not live in
RAM. The authoring is the first bill, never the last.

What I still want is the tooling talk. How do you keep a clip findable at that
scale? Nothing published so far says. Probably a GDC session in 2028.
