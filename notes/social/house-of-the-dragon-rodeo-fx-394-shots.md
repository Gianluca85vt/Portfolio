## LinkedIn

Rodeo FX finally put a number on its House of the Dragon season 3: 394 shots, six CG dragons, and roughly a hundred crowd shots inside the Battle of Tumbleton alone.

One line in that breakdown is worth more than the shot count. Silverwing — the dragon that carries the finale — was designed and animated by Rodeo FX for season 2. She was rebuilt for nothing. She was reused.

That is how a six-dragon season gets paid for. Six dragons on screen and six dragons in production are two different numbers, and the gap between them is the only reason the first number is possible. Build one hero creature properly once — skeleton, wing membrane, scale layout, shaders that hold from a mile out to a close-up — and the cost lands in one season while the value spreads across several.

Anyone who has inherited an asset knows where the bill arrives, though. New shots mean new camera distances, and a membrane that read fine in a season 2 wide will show its displacement at half the range. New lighting means re-tuning shaders. New choreography puts the rig in poses nobody tested, and something in the shoulder breaks. Reuse saves you the first eighty per cent.

The genuinely expensive frames are elsewhere. About a hundred crowd shots layered with dragonfire and large-scale destruction simulation means three systems in one shot, each of which wants to run last. Destruction needs the fire's forces to know what to push. Fire needs the geometry destruction produced, or it burns through a wall that is no longer standing. The crowd has to flinch at the right frame and fall in the right direction once the roof has gone. Change any one and every cache downstream goes stale.

Which is why Rodeo FX's FX supervisor titled his Houdini HIVE talk last month around workflow. With the shot count public, that word reads differently.

Two of the season's eight vendors have now published anything at all. That is a start on the record, not the record.
