## LinkedIn

Every studio has had this argument. Somebody suggests moving to Blender, somebody who has used 3ds Max since university says no, and the reason is never really about features.

It is about the fifty things a day you do without looking. The quad menu under the right mouse button. Alt-W to maximise a viewport. The modifier stack you read top to bottom like a sentence. Twenty years of that lives in your hands, not your head, and asking an artist to switch DCC is asking them to be slow at their own job for six months.

Jake Denham has used 3ds Max for eighteen years and got tired of being told to switch. So he spent a week vibe-coding Max on top of Blender: the hotkeys, the quad menu, the modifier stack. A shrewd choice of three, because those are the three things a Max user's hands actually know. He reports a cold start of 14.8 seconds against Max's 57.0 on the same machine, and says it opens his real .max files with no 3ds Max installed — 23 of 23 old scenes.

That last claim is the one I would want independently checked. .max has never had a public specification, and a production scene file is a container holding whatever every plugin that touched it decided to write. Nobody outside his machine has opened those files, and since the project is a personal one with no download, nobody will.

The part that does not need verifying is the hour count. One artist, one week, no C++. A UI compatibility layer has always been obvious and never worth a TD's time, because the payoff is one department's comfort. It is worth a week now.

Which does not make Max a dead tool — Tyson Ibele is still shipping real engineering into it. The lock-in was always the hands, plus the plugins, plus the farm, plus the client who wants a .max file. One week got the hands. The hands were only ever the cheap part.
