Land of the Lustrous is the show people cite to prove CG anime can be
beautiful. Studio Orange gets the credit, and Orange earned it. But a good part
of that result was settled years earlier at a drawing desk, when Haruko
Ichikawa decided her cast would be made of gems.

Look at a gem as geometry. Hard surface. Flat facets that catch a highlight and
hold it. Translucency, which a renderer solves with subsurface scattering every
frame, with nobody hand-painting the falloff. Hair that is crystal, so it gets
modelled as a solid and never has to be simulated as hair. A smooth,
near-mannequin skull under the face, which keeps the topology clean enough to
reuse the model across a whole series. That is a list of things 3D does cheaply
and hand-drawn animation pays dearly for. The source material arrived already
shaped like what the pipeline wanted.

Kodansha has now announced Ichikawa's next series. Cosmic Mimimi starts in
Monthly Afternoon on 25 November 2026, billed as "neo kawaii sci-fi", and
coverage of the announcement visual describes designs running from humanoid
figures through to creatures shaped like caterpillars and springs.

Those two words land differently if you have ever rigged one. A caterpillar is
a long joint chain with skin that must bulge where it bunches and thin where it
stretches, and keeping the mesh from creasing is days of weight painting. A
spring is worse, because a spring's whole character is that it refuses to
conserve its silhouette. Kawaii as a drawing idiom runs on that licence: a
cheek balloons for one frame and snaps back before anyone counts the mass. A
pencil does that for free. A mesh has a volume and keeps it until somebody
builds the machinery to make it lie — which is exactly the hand-work Orange has
spent years on, and exactly the work Lustrous let them skip.

No adaptation has been announced and I am not predicting one. The point is
narrower: what an artist chooses to draw decides, years ahead, which production
methods can follow her.
