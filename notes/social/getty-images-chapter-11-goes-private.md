## LinkedIn

Getty Images filed for Chapter 11 on 21 October. Most coverage led with AI. Getty's own filing leads with warrants, a dead merger and debt service.

Both are true, and the gap between them is interesting.

The numbers: a prepackaged filing in Delaware, up to $533 million of new equity from Getty family affiliates and Koch Equity Development, full ownership on the other side, existing shares cancelled, more than $500 million cleared against roughly $1.46 billion in funded debt. Hearing on 14 December, completion targeted for year end. Getty says operations and contributor payments continue in the ordinary course.

The part Getty's lawyers emphasise, writing for a judge, is non-operational: judgments in warrant litigation, the Shutterstock merger that the UK CMA would only clear if Shutterstock sold its editorial business, and the debt. Getty walked away from that deal on 30 June after refusing the condition — having spent, by its own account, over $100 million in professional and financing fees getting there.

What it means at my desk is simpler. A stock library does three jobs for anyone building environments: reference that is dated, captioned and shot by someone who was standing there; editorial imagery that survives a legal read; and indemnity, which is the actual product. A generative model competes on the first and is useless on the other two. It will give you a plausible 1970s switchgear panel in four seconds. It will not give you the one installed in 1974, and it will not give you anyone to point at when a rights holder writes in.

The thing worth watching is governance. A listed company has to publish what it pays contributors and explain a change in terms. A company owned by a family office and a private investment arm does not. If rates move in 2027, the first anyone hears of it will be an email.

Same unanswered question as Unity promising Asset Store artists a cut of Spark without writing down the rate. The commitment is public. The number is the agreement.

The debt: roughly $1.46bn funded, more than $500m cleared by the plan. Holders of 83.5% of the unsecured notes agreed to retire $270m of them. Getty expects to be done by year end.

Worth remembering: Nov 2025, UK High Court. Getty dropped its core training-copyright claim mid-trial. Secondary infringement dismissed. It won a watermark point the judge called "extremely limited in scope". The company with the archive and the lawyers got no ruling.
