## LinkedIn

Nicalis shipped a free Grand Theft Auto parody mode into The Binding of Isaac:
Repentance+ on GTA 6's launch day, with a soundtrack of its own. Players named the
music as AI-generated over the weekend. By Monday the publisher had announced the
"removal of generated music files". Four days, start to finish.

The part worth looking at is not the backlash. It is which asset class this happened
to, because it is the same one every time.

A prop crosses several desks before it reaches a retail build: reference, a modeller,
a tech-art check on UVs and texel budget, art direction on the silhouette, a lighting
pass that finds out it was never meant to be seen from that angle. Ten people have had
it on screen. A music file goes from a folder into the build. Audio gets checked once,
by one person in headphones, usually late, and when the schedule tightens the bar it
clears is "does it play".

Generated music also arrives as a finished stereo master, and that single fact causes
most of the tells players picked up on. No separable stems, so nothing can be ducked
under a boss sting or thinned out for a quiet room. Loop seams that were never written
as seams, which matters in a game that keeps one track running for a whole floor. Even,
compressed loudness that gives it away next to a human master before the melody does.

Isaac is a bad game to try this in. The community has argued about its soundtrack since
Ridiculon — Matthias Bossi and Jon Evans — took over from Danny Baranowsky for Rebirth
in 2014. Those are people who can name their favourite floor theme.

And it landed in a joke mode, which is where standards always relax: nobody commissions
a composer for a week-long parody. That is exactly why it is the useful place to look.
A standard that only covers the work a studio is proud of is a marketing position.

A library licence for four loops costs less than a week of negative Steam reviews. The
files are gone. The score stays on the page.
