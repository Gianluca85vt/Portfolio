Bleach finished today. Twenty-two years, 366 episodes of the original run from 2004, then fifty more across the four parts of Thousand-Year Blood War. The last two of those fifty arrived four weeks later than scheduled, and the production committee's stated reason was quality as the story reached its climax.

That delay is worth reading as production information rather than as a disappointment.

A fifty-episode run is not fifty equally difficult episodes. In a shonen adaptation the curve bends upward hard at the end, and it bends in exactly the departments you cannot staff your way out of. Episodes 49 and 50 of this arc are final-form fights: effects animation drawn frame by frame by a small number of specialists, then photography, where those elements get layered, glowed, distorted and graded into the shot. Both are per-cut costs. Put six animators on one cut of a getsuga and you get six slightly different getsugas plus a supervisor's week spent reconciling them.

The weekly slot, meanwhile, is still twenty-two minutes. Same as episode one.

So the overrun shows up at the end by construction. Episodes are made roughly in order, the buffer gets spent on whatever went wrong in the middle, and the most expensive episodes in the production meet the schedule at its thinnest. September's notice was not when the problem began — it was when there was nowhere left to hide it.

What Studio Pierrot and the committee did instead is the standard instrument: four weeks of reruns in the slot. It costs almost nothing. The slot keeps its habit with the audience, the ad inventory still sells, and the staff get four weeks they would otherwise have taken out of their own nights. An episode that ships under-composited is the one people link to for twenty years. A four-week wait is forgotten by December.

The part I would still like answered: "to improve quality" names no department and no cause, so the people who absorbed that overtime stay invisible.
