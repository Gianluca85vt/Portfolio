## LinkedIn

Nintendo published the 24th Ask the Developer on 2 November, three days before Ocarina of Time lands on Switch 2, and the reason the remake exists turns out to be a staffing one.

Eiji Aonuma says most of the current Zelda team has only worked on recent games, and he wondered whether they understood why the 1998 one mattered. So he had them rebuild it. Then he deliberately let the younger staff decide which elements needed revising — and he was the dungeon designer on the original, so he is the one person with the standing to overrule them.

For anyone who works on remakes, that is the whole problem in one decision.

The original's look was not a free choice. The Nintendo 64 gave you roughly 4KB of texture memory, which is why those surfaces are so flat and why vertex colour is doing work a texture map would do today. Fog was draw distance. Hyrule Field was empty because the machine could not fill it.

Players took all of it as mood. The fog became atmosphere, the emptiness became scale.

So the rebuild has to separate two things that look identical from outside: a decision made for the art, and a decision made for the cartridge. Keep the fog and you preserve a budget workaround as art direction. Lift it and you may delete the reason the place felt like anything. A team raised on Breath of the Wild has no instinct for which is which — and Aonuma is betting that is an advantage.

Blizzard hit a version of this with the classic WoW model rebuild: the pushback was never about texture quality, it landed on silhouettes moving a few degrees. The silhouette is what memory stores.

Nintendo EPD Production Group No. 3 built this one, with HokWai Ng directing. Previews call the visuals stunning and disagree about how much of Hyrule Field moved. That disagreement is the thing I will be reading reviews for on Thursday.
