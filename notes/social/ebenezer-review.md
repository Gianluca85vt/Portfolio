## LinkedIn

Six critics gave Ti West's Ebenezer a number I could read. They average 7.0 and
they run from 5 to 9. The wider critic body is colder than that — Rotten
Tomatoes sat at 53% from 116 reviews on 14 November, Metacritic at 46 from 32.

What makes it worth writing about is where the agreement sits. Nobody is
arguing about the ghosts.

Empire describes a Ghost of Christmas Past with a hint of corpse about it, and
Ignorance and Want climbing out of a ribcage. RogerEbert.com's headline is
huzzah to West, humbug to Depp. Even the hostile reviews stop to praise the
spirit work.

There is a production decision underneath that praise, and it is a frugal one.

The default screen ghost is translucent — soft falloff, displaced edges, light
passing through. That is a compositing job from start to finish. Nobody on set
sees it, the actor plays to a tennis ball, and the shot does not exist until a
comp artist builds it, which means it can be re-noted indefinitely and usually
is.

A rotting ghost is buildable. Decay is texture, texture goes in front of a lens,
and the camera and the actor both react to something that is actually in the
room. You still pay for cleanup and wire removal, but the shot is mostly
finished when the camera stops. For a film whose money clearly did not go into
a full spirit pipeline, that is the right trade, and the reviews are the
receipt.

The 35mm origination is the same instinct. Grain sits over fog and gaslight for
free, and the negative exists afterwards — which matters more than anyone
making a Christmas film in 2026 is thinking about.

Where it comes apart is Depp. And the tell, reading across the reviews, is how
often the makeup turns up in the sentence before the performance does. Heavy
prosthetics on a lead are a bet that the audience stops seeing them within ten
minutes. When critics are still describing the build in the back half of a
review, that bet has not paid.

Second Film & TV review in a week where the departments delivered and the
middle of the film did not.
