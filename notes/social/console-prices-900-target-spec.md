## LinkedIn

A PS5 Pro is $900 and a base PS5 renders Marvel's Wolverine at 864p before the upscaler touches it.

Those are the same decision, filed in two different departments.

Circana's US figures through August: PlayStation hardware units down 25% year on year, the worst position since 2013. Xbox hardware down 33%, an all-time low across twenty-five years. Circana's own phrase for the market is "most precarious since the 1980s". Microsoft has put Xbox prices up three times in two years. Sony never did the mid-generation cut that every previous PlayStation got.

The install base is shrinking. The fidelity target the art teams build against has not moved since 2020.

I have never once been handed a target spec that went down. A price rise becomes a smaller forecast, which becomes a tighter budget, and that lands on the art team as a schedule. The spec never reopens, because it was agreed the day the trailer was approved. So the slack comes out of the picture: internal resolution down, and a network hired to make the difference look plausible. QSSR does that in 1.5 to 1.8ms on a base PS5 — a tenth of a 60fps frame, taken straight back out of shadow resolution or draw distance.

Gears of War: E-Day is the first shipping game on Epic's MegaLights. Roughly three shadow casters in view became hundreds, and The Coalition did lovely work with it. Only a 5090 clears native 4K60. Alinea estimates around 230,000 copies sold.

Fuse Games did the other thing on Star Wars Galactic Racer. 60Hz physics tick, 60fps floor on every box including Series S. An art budget written around the weakest machine three years out, and held there every time somebody asked for one more light.

Tell me the box is $900 and the audience is shrinking and I will light something beautiful inside those walls. That is the job and it always was. What I cannot do is hit a number set when the market was a different shape and call the upscaler even.
