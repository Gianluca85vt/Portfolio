## LinkedIn

Jagex announced a fourth RuneScape MMO at RuneFest on Saturday. Working title RS4, built in Unreal Engine, a few years out. Most of the coverage read the engine line as RuneScape finally leaving its own tech behind, and that part already happened.

RuneScape: Dragonwilds shipped 1.0 on 15 September. It has been an Unreal Engine 5 project since 2022, and it spent a year and a half in early access. So Jagex is not adopting Unreal for RS4. It is continuing on an engine it has already shipped a RuneScape game on, with the tooling and the art pipeline that came with it. For a studio whose last mainline game is over a decade old, that removes the worst sequel risk there is: a team learning an engine and a game simultaneously.

What is genuinely new is the MMO part, and that is a different engineering problem from the one Dragonwilds solved. Dragonwilds is one to four players in a private session. An MMO is hundreds of clients on one authoritative world, with replication, interest management and persistence meant to survive a decade of players testing it. Unreal's networking was shaped around shooter-sized sessions, and studios building MMOs on it have historically had to go in and rework that layer.

The art problem interests me more. RuneScape's look survives because it reads small and at distance — strong silhouettes, flat colour separation, a world you can parse while you are watching a skill bar instead of the scenery. Unreal's defaults pull toward a photographic surface response. Dragonwilds suggests Jagex can hold a stylised look on this engine. Holding it with forty players on screen, each in their own gear, under a full day cycle, is the test nobody has footage of yet.

The teaser is pre-rendered concept work. Nobody outside Jagex has seen the renderer.
