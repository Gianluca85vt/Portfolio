Manchuria Opium Squad came back to Weekly Young Magazine this week, eleven months after its artist died. Shikako was 37. The credit box now reads: story by Tsukasa Monma, art by Tsurushima, original art by Shikako.

The interesting part of that announcement is one clause about who Tsurushima is. He and Shikako worked as assistants on the same series — Yasuhisa Hara's Kingdom — and Tsurushima was the senior of the two.

Kingdom is a crowd book. Armies, lacquered armour, dust, siege works. Nobody draws that alone and nobody ever has. What an assistant learns on a series like that is a set of answers that never gets written down: how heavy the line goes at this distance, how much hatching a shadow takes before it reads as mud, where to stop because the eye fills in the rest. Those answers get corrected out of you over years until your hand produces them without being asked.

So when a publisher needs somebody to carry a dead artist's look, they do not go looking for the best available draughtsman. They go looking for someone who sat at the same desk.

I keep thinking about how differently we handle this in 3D. We document. Master materials, naming conventions, shading guidelines with roughness ranges, style boards, rigs with locked controls. All of it helps. And a character still comes back subtly wrong the first month after the person who built the look leaves, because documentation holds decisions and not the judgement that produced them. The doc says roughness 0.3 to 0.45 on worn steel. It does not say when to break that because the shot is backlit and the hero needs to separate.

Manga skips the documentation layer and transfers the only part that actually moves: a person who absorbed the judgement. Worse system in every respect except that one.

The same issue announced an anime adaptation. Pony Canyon has a site and nothing else so far — no studio, no staff, no date — which means whoever gets it will be building character sheets from two artists' drawings at once and deciding which of them to follow.

Chapter 217 is where the hand changes.
