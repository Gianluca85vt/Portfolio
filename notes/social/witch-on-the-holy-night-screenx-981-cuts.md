## LinkedIn

ufotable has widened 981 cuts of its Witch on the Holy Night film for ScreenX. If you work in 2D animation, that number should stop you, because drawn animation has no outside the frame.

ScreenX, CJ 4DPLEX's format, projects onto the left and right walls of the auditorium as well as the main screen — about 270 degrees of coverage. For live action it is a solved post problem. The negative is wider than the frame that got used, second-unit material usually exists, and a plate can be extended with the same tools that paint out a boom mic. There is image outside the crop.

A background painting has no such margin. It was made to the layout, the layout was drawn to the frame, and it stops there, because paint costs money and nobody was ever going to see the edge. Widening a cut means a background artist painting more of a place that was never designed past its own border, in the original's style, long after the department moved on. Doing it 981 times is a production of its own.

What makes ufotable able to absorb it is the thing their camera moves have always depended on: drawn layers riding real 3D geometry. A set exists in the scene file. When the frame needs to be wider, you move the frustum and render again. Studios that bake their look into flat background paint cannot do that.

The figure I keep coming back to is 67 minutes of side content out of a 129-minute film. Roughly half. Mahoyo is a TYPE-MOON visual novel, which means long stretches of two people in a room talking, and there is nothing to put on a side wall during a close-up. Leaving those panels dark is the honest answer. Half this film is where that answer lives.

The open question is the grade. ufotable's look is diffusion and glow, and an auditorium wall has no black level — project a bloom-heavy comp onto architecture at an acute angle and the contrast collapses toward grey. The side panels need their own pass, not the main comp widened.

Premium formats from 27 November in Japan. International from 21 January 2027.
