Ubisoft put out three pieces of news on 19 November. 166 jobs cut at Ubisoft Montreal and Ubisoft Quebec, two of its five Creative Houses merged into one, and a studio founded that is dedicated to generative AI in games.

Three separate announcements. I am not going to claim one caused another, because nothing published says so and the numbers do not line up that way. But read together they describe where a publisher is adding structure and where it has decided structure is no longer warranted.

Push Square put the cut at roughly four per cent of Ubisoft's Canadian workforce. Four per cent is how it reads on a payroll. A pipeline does not lose people by percentage.

An Assassin's Creed is a content factory, and what makes a content factory function is not a design document. It is a few hundred people who agree, without ever discussing it, on how an asset gets named, which LOD budget a façade is allowed, how the streaming grid is carved up, and which of the four tools that can place a prop is the one you actually use for a roof. That consensus took fifteen years to form in Montreal. It lives in habit and in the hands of people who have been there long enough to have opinions about it.

Ubisoft said the affected teams were on projects that were cancelled or refocused. That selects for exactly the seniority holding the consensus, because senior people are the ones moved onto the speculative work that later gets cut. The throughput loss does not show up this quarter. It shows up two years out, in an open world that takes longer to populate than the last one did, and nobody can explain why.

The other detail worth keeping: the Creative House structure was announced in January 2026 and meant to start in April. Two of the five are already merged. A reorganisation that reduces its own unit count in eight months is telling you the count was a plan rather than a finding.
