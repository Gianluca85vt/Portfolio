A soulslike asks you to read a wind-up — which arm, which arc, how many frames before it lands. Third person gives you three things for free that make that readable: the enemy's whole silhouette in frame, your own body as a reference for reach, and a camera far enough back that an attack begins and ends inside the picture.

Valor Mortis, which One More Level shipped on 13 October, throws all three away. It is a first-person soulslike built around a rapier and a parry window.

That is a harder animation brief than it sounds. In first person there is no player body, so nothing calibrates reach. The field of view is narrower, so a wide swing starts outside the frame. And the enemy stands close enough that their torso fills the screen while the arm doing the damage is somewhere past the edge of it. The silhouette, which is the whole UI of third-person combat, stops being available.

What is left is a short list of channels. The upper body, held a beat longer than you would hold it with the camera back. Audio — a scrape, an intake of breath, a footstep landing on a different surface. Weapon flash. And the one affordance first person hands you, which is that the enemy's eyeline points at the player rather than at an avatar.

The reviews run from 6 to 9, a three-point spread on a ten-point scale, and the parry is the thing both ends of it agree works. Seven verdicts I could verify average 7.5. The points came off elsewhere — scripted parkour, boss platforming, a bestiary that runs out.

When a game divides critics that widely and the disagreement skips the combat entirely, the animation team solved the problem they set themselves. The rest of the budget shows where it did not stretch.
