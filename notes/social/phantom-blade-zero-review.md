## LinkedIn

Phantom Blade Zero's reviews landed this morning with a four-point spread, and the gap is more useful than the average.

Nine scored verdicts I could verify come out at 8.5. PCGamesN gave it a 10. Eurogamer gave it 3 out of 5. Metacritic read 88 from 31 reviews; OpenCritic read 91 from 24. Everybody agrees on the combat — the deflect-and-parry chain is the reason for every high score, and GameSpot said outright that it is good enough to stop you caring that the story makes no sense.

What interests me is a technical artist's problem hiding inside the PC verdicts.

Wccftech measured about 100fps at 4K High with DLSS Quality on a 4080, high 50s with ray tracing, roughly 30 with path tracing. A clean curve. Glitched then reported frame-pacing problems and camera stutter on a 5080 at maximum settings — while averaging around 60fps.

Both of those can be true, and that is the issue. An average of 60 tells you nothing about whether frames arrive 16.7ms apart or in clumps. S-GAME has built a game whose entire combat system asks you to press a button inside a window of a few frames. In that design, frame-time consistency is not a graphics setting. It is the mechanic. Almost nobody published frame-time captures, so the scores went out on feel.

Then the Steam Deck: Steam Deck HQ could not hold 30fps at the lowest settings, averaging 23 to 27 and dipping to 10 in one area. Back in June, S-GAME said Unreal Engine 5 would not be a problem and that the game would run well on weak hardware. I have read enough of those statements to know what they usually describe — a vertical slice profiled before the real content lands, before foliage density climbs and streaming volumes start overlapping. Sincere when made, worthless by ship date.

The combat looks genuinely excellent. The measurement that would tell you whether it feels excellent on your machine has not been taken.
