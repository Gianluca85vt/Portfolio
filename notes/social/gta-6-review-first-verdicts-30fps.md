## LinkedIn

GTA 6's review embargo lifted this morning and the first seven scored verdicts I could verify are all perfect. GameSpot, GamesRadar+, TechRadar, VGC, Creative Bloq, Screen Rant, Inven Global. Average 10, range zero.

That number will move. It is seven outlets, hours after the gates opened, and a Rockstar first wave is self-selecting: the people with code weeks early are the people who could finish an eighty-hour game before launch. Metacritic and OpenCritic had no aggregate live when I checked. Ask again in a week.

The part worth an artist's attention is underneath the score, and every review says the same thing. Thirty frames per second, locked, on base PS5 and PS5 Pro alike. No performance mode. No 40fps option on a 120Hz panel.

Hitting 30 is a spreadsheet exercise. Holding it is not. GamesRadar+ reviewed on a Pro and reports no fluctuation at all; Creative Bloq calls the lock stable with controls that feel right underneath it. In a city where a player can turn one corner into an entirely different density of world, flat frame times mean somebody at Rockstar set a streaming budget in year two and spent four more years refusing to let the art team past it. That is the discipline nobody puts in a trailer, and it is the first thing to go on most productions.

One detail I keep turning over: GamesRadar+ only saw pop-in in sessions running beyond eight hours. Draw-distance problems do not wait eight hours — they show up the first time you drive east at sunset. A fault that needs that long to surface looks more like an allocator or a streaming cache slowly losing to fragmentation. Can't prove it from outside. Nobody who can is talking.

And GameSpot's review carries three bylines. You do not split a review three ways unless one person could not see enough of the game in the time available.
