## LinkedIn

ABEMA just commissioned three anime pilots chosen from a public open call. Each winner delivers four minutes of finished animation rather than a twelve-episode series.

Announced on 6 November, under CyberAgent's Project PRISMation. The three are OXY-HEAVEN by Waboku, Amneka by a creator working as orange, and Kandy-Killers for Hire by US-based illustrator and animator Dominique Garrow. All dated 2027 or later. The call had no professional requirement and no condition on age or nationality.

The precedent is the reason it exists. PRISMation's first pilot, Poppin-Play Kitchen, ran four and a half minutes, was made by a creator in Thailand, and had 2.13 million YouTube views by mid-May on ABEMA's own count. It got a sequel.

As someone who works in a pipeline, the list of what ABEMA says it provides is the most informative line in the announcement: development, voice casting, music, translation, promotion. That is an inventory of what a solo animator cannot buy. A casting director, a composer, a localisation pass, and an audience. The drawing is the part they already have.

A four-minute pilot tests things a trailer never has to. A trailer can be cut from the best twenty seconds of anything. A pilot has to hold a scene: designs that survive being turned, layout, in-betweens, colour, compositing, a mix, voices that match mouths. Every department of a series at a hundredth of the length, usually on one or two people. Months of evenings.

What nobody has published is the money, the delivery window, or the view count that turns a pilot into a series. One greenlight is a result, not a rule. I would not read 2.13 million as the bar.

Commissioning shorts from outside the studio system also competes for none of the studio weeks nobody has spare. That may be the quietest argument in favour of it.
