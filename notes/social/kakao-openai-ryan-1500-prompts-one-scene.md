## LinkedIn

Kakao says one scene of its new AI-generated short took about 1,500 prompts. It has not said how many scenes the film has.

The film is "Still, Ryan", five to seven minutes depending on which outlet you read, made with OpenAI and released on 11 November 2026 across Kakao Friends' and OpenAI's own channels. Han Ji-won directs, off the Netflix animated feature "Lost in Starlight". Kim Young-jun is credited as AI supervisor. The tool, per the coverage, is ChatGPT's image feature.

That last detail is the one worth sitting with. A still-image generator, not a video model. The described pipeline runs sketch, generate, select, hand-edit — which means the published account says nothing about where the motion came from. Inbetweening is the part of animation that actually costs money, and it is absent from the press release.

Then there is the character. Ryan is a lion with no mane, and that absence is his entire design and his entire arc. Hand that to a model whose prior for "lion" is built from a few million maned lions and the mane comes back every roll — a wisp at the jawline, a fuller crown, a rim light that reads as fur. Never broken enough to reject outright, always wrong enough to break the design, and different every frame because the tool holds no memory of the frame before.

So 1,500 attempts on one scene is entirely believable. I would expect it.

What I keep coming back to is that a key animator knows after the first drawing whether the design is holding, because they drew it, and the second drawing inherits that decision. The generated version relitigates the character from scratch on every roll. Fifteen hundred of those is somebody at a desk all day doing quality control on a slot machine.

Kakao published a prompt count with no scene count, no shot count, no crew size and no schedule. Those are the figures that would turn an announcement into evidence.
