## LinkedIn

Modern Warfare 4's reviews landed on 16 October, a week before the game ships, and every one of them covers about a third of it. Activision gave digital pre-orders the campaign and nothing else, so the five outlets that put a number on it scored sixteen missions and six to nine hours. GameSpot 7, IGN 8 with a "not final" tag, But Why Tho? 8.5, Insider Gaming 9, GamesBeat's reviewer at 4.5 out of 5. Mean of 8.5, range of two points.

What interests me is where the two points sit. All five land on the same stretch: a semi-open, behind-enemy-lines mission borrowed from DMZ. IGN calls it a highlight. GamesBeat says it is where the pacing goes.

From the outside that reads like a budget rather than a taste. A linear Call of Duty mission is authored for one route — the art team knows where the camera will be, so every silhouette, every light, every piece of set dressing gets placed for that single read. Let the player approach from any direction and you cannot do that any more. You build a kit instead: modular shells, instanced props, lighting that has to work from all sides. Cheaper per square kilometre, thinner per minute. Players feel the difference as pacing even when they call it design.

The number I would actually keep is a different one. This is the first Modern Warfare to skip PS4 and Xbox One, and Warzone left those machines in June. For thirteen years a Call of Duty art budget had a Jaguar CPU and roughly 5GB of usable memory underneath it, because the campaign had to run there. That floor is gone. What replaces it is Switch 2, day one, built in-house by Infinity Ward with Digital Legends, with Beenox on PC — which is a very different thing from a port handed outside after the fact.

Nobody has measured the Switch 2 build yet. That is the number worth waiting for.
