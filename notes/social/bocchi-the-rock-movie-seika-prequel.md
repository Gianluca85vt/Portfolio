A Bocchi the Rock! prequel film was announced on Sunday, and the production
question it raises is more interesting than the news itself.

Quick facts: subtitled Hoshi no Ashiato, Japanese cinemas in 2027, adapting the
manga's prologue story about Seika, Nijika's older sister. Maaya Uchida plays
Seika. Shin Wakabayashi, of Wonder Egg Priority, directs.

Here is why that matters to anyone who has worked on a stylised production. The
2022 series earned its reputation on two things. One is performance animation
that puts fingers on the correct frets for the chord you are hearing, which is
slow, reference-heavy work that almost nothing else in television anime pays
for. The other is register switching — claymation, cut paper, photo collage,
crayon, live-action spliced in for two seconds. Every one of those switches is
motivated by one character's interior panic. That anchor is what stops them
reading as a gag reel.

Seika does not panic. She runs the live house and keeps her face still. So a
film about her takes the engine out of the vocabulary, and the production has to
either invent a new motivation for breaking the frame or play it straight and
give up the thing audiences clip.

Add the handover. Keiichirou Saitou directed the series at CloverWorks and set
all of this. Season 2 went to his assistant director, Yusuke Yamamoto. The film
goes to Wakabayashi, who was on neither. A style assembled from one-off
techniques is the hardest kind to hand over, because every switch is a bespoke
shot somebody has to invent, schedule and composite. There is no library to
inherit.

A feature is the right container for that bet, though. Fixed delivery, one
deadline, more hours per cut than a weekly episode, no twelfth-week wall — which
is exactly what Wonder Egg Priority ran out of.
