Dragon's Dogma 2: Dark Arisen landed today with an average of 8.5 across the eight scored verdicts I could verify, and a spread from 7 to 10. That spread is the interesting part of the release, because it is not spread evenly. Read the low reviews next to the high ones and they agree about the new northern region, the story and the combat. They disagree about one system: the randomised Relic gear.

From an art pipeline that disagreement makes sense. Dragon's Dogma 2 authors its armour as discrete sets. A cuirass has a modelled buckle, a weight that reads in the walk cycle, a material response somebody tuned against the game's lighting. There are not many of them, and that scarcity is doing social work: recognising a piece of armour on another player's pawn is part of how the Rift functions. Bolt randomised affixes on top and the same cuirass exists in forty numerical variants. The player stops reading it as an object and starts reading it as a roll, and the silhouette an artist spent weeks on becomes a stat line.

The other half of this release is the Switch 2 version, and that is the harder engineering. Dragon's Dogma 2 in 2024 was CPU-bound, falling to the low thirties in its capital on a PS5 because every NPC in Vernworth ran a schedule. Capcom's producer now says the Switch 2 build holds a 30fps floor and often runs higher, with frame generation producing a 60fps-equivalent feel. Generated frames interpolate what the renderer already drew. They do not make the simulation tick faster, and they do not shorten the path from a thumb to a parry. In a game built on reading a wind-up and answering inside a window, that gap sits where the player can feel it.

Which frame generation is still unclear: Vice reports AMD's FSR, other coverage describes a DLSS pipeline, and Capcom has not published internal render resolutions. Worth watching when someone frame-counts it properly.

Dragon's Dogma 2: Dark Arisen averages 8.5 across the eight verdicts I could verify today, range 7 to 10. The spread is not random — the low and high reviews agree on the region, the story and the combat, and split on one system.

That system is the randomised Relic gear. You find items, pay Rift Crystals to appraise them, and get rolled passive effects on top of hand-authored armour sets.

Why that fights the game: Dogma authors armour as discrete pieces. Modelled buckles, weight that reads in the walk cycle, materials tuned against the lighting. Scarcity is the point — you recognise a set on another player's pawn.

Forty numerical variants of the same cuirass and the player stops reading an object and starts reading a roll. Diablo can afford that; its armour is authored for a 2-inch silhouette at thirty degrees. Dogma's is authored for third person at walking pace.

The Switch 2 build is the harder work. DD2 was CPU-bound in 2024 — low thirties in its capital on a PS5, every NPC running a schedule. Capcom's producer says Switch 2 now holds a 30fps floor and often beats it.

Frame gen on top gives a 60fps-equivalent look. It does not speed the simulation or shorten thumb-to-parry latency. 30fps sim presented at 60 is a smoother picture of the same 33ms decision grid — in a parry game, that is felt.

Unsettled: Vice reports FSR frame generation, other coverage describes DLSS, and Capcom has published no internal render resolutions. Somebody needs to frame-count it.
