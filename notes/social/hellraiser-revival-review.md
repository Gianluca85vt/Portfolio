## LinkedIn

Clive Barker's Hellraiser: Revival launched today, and the six scored reviews I
could verify run from 4.5 to 8.5. Four points of a ten-point scale. The average
is 7.5, which is the least useful number in this whole piece.

What makes it interesting is that the critics are not arguing. Read the reviews
side by side and they agree on nearly everything. GameSpot called it the best
thing to carry the Hellraiser name since the 1987 film. CGMagazine called it one
of the best licensed games ever made. Game Informer gave it 4.5 and went after
the bugs. All of them praise the Cenobite design, the gore and the sound. All of
them say the combat does not work.

So the spread is a weighting disagreement: how much a broken combat loop should
cost a game that looks this good. In one case it is also about hardware. The 4.5
is a PS5 review built largely around technical problems, while the top scores
came from PC.

The detail I keep coming back to is the minimum spec. A GTX 1660 and 8GB of RAM,
and 80GB of install. A modest compute floor with an enormous footprint tells you
where the months went: texture resolution, audio and authored sequences, rather
than systems that need a fast GPU to evaluate. You can read a production's
priorities out of a minimum spec more often than people expect, and here it
matches what the reviews describe from the other side.

This is Saber's business — a slate of other people's licences. Hellraiser,
Turok, Jurassic Park, John Wick. A licence hands you a creature canon somebody
else already solved and a visual language your concept artists do not have to
invent. Hit feedback and weapon weight you build from scratch — the half the
reviews are unanimous about.

The studio credits are genuinely muddled across sources, for the record: Saber,
Mad Head Games and Boss Team Games are attributed differently depending on who
you read.
