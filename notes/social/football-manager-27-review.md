The most consequential piece of work in Football Manager 27 is that Sports Interactive made the dots smaller. Said plainly, because that is what the year bought.

The official feature post on the 2D match view says the ball and the player discs were scaled down so they sit more believably on the pitch, with the shadows redone and the net made less opaque. Somebody spent real time on that. It is the clearest signal of what went wrong last year.

Football Manager 26 holds the lowest Metacritic score in the series — 68 from 44 critics — and its Steam user reviews settled around 22% positive. That is interface damage. Nobody walks away from a management sim over defensive line height. They go because the screen they look at four hundred times a season got worse, and FM26's interface, rebuilt for the series' move to Unity, was that screen.

A precision point, since it gets reported loosely: at SEGA and Sports Interactive, Unity was a presentation and platform change. The simulation underneath stayed on SI's own systems, where it has always lived. The rewritten part broke. The part they left alone did not.

FM27's four published verdicts average 8.0, from 7.5 to 8.9, on a thin early field — Metacritic showed 82 from seven critics on 8 November, two days ahead of release. Cubed3 wrote the most useful sentence anyone has managed about this game: 8 compared with FM26, no higher than 5 compared with the entries before it.

What holds my attention as a technical artist is where the effort landed. Not the 3D crowd. Contrast at the goal line, disc scale against the pitch markings, shadow separation — the unglamorous readability pass that decides whether a data display can be read at a glance. Nobody cuts a trailer out of that work, and everybody notices when it is missing.

Two development cycles to get back to a line they had already crossed. The interface team earned the credit. It still cost two years.
