## LinkedIn

Grand Theft Auto 6 installs at around 212 gigabytes.

On Sunday EA put out a message asking people not to delete Madden NFL 27 to make room for it.

A 1TB PS5 leaves you 840GB once the system takes its share. The older 825GB machine leaves 667. One game takes a quarter of the first and nearly a third of the second, before the day-one patch and an online mode that will grow for a decade.

That is a publisher fighting for residency on your drive, and it was the most honest thing said all week. A game you uninstall is a game you don't go back to, and everyone running a live service knows that number exactly.

The Coalition is spending the same hardware differently. Gears of War: E-Day pages cold city data out to the Series S SSD and treats it as a slower tier of RAM, buying back about a gigabyte against an eight gigabyte budget. Tom's Hardware clocked the PC version at 3.7 GB/s. Lovely work. It is also an authoring problem in an engineering hat: what goes cold together is a content decision, made in the art directory.

Same component. Warehouse for one game, working memory for another, inside a budget that stopped growing the day the console shipped.

I know where 212 gigabytes comes from. BC7 at 4K across a few thousand materials. Audio at a bitrate somebody won in month four. The same brick wall written into eleven streaming packages, because reading it linearly beats seeking for it.

And the size pass always lands six weeks from ship, where it comes out of surfaces. A batch job dropping whole directories two tiers, with an exceptions list you argue one at a time by Friday. Nobody removes a district that late.

Analysts have Take-Two at 55 million copies by March, blended price near $90. Five billion dollars, and a quarter of every drive it lands on. The space costs nobody anything, so it goes to whoever can take it, and the bill gets paid by whatever gets uninstalled to clear the room.
