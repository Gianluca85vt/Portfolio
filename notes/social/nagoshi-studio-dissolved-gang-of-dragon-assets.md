## LinkedIn

Nagoshi Studio was dissolved by a shareholders' resolution on 31 October. The notice turned up in Japan's government gazette on 2 November. No statement, no farewell post, no games shipped.

Toshihiro Nagoshi left Sega's Ryu Ga Gotoku Studio — Yakuza, Like a Dragon — and set up again with NetEase backing. GANG OF DRAGON was revealed at The Game Awards in December 2025 and is now gone, ten months later.

The reported numbers explain the business decision easily enough. Bloomberg's reporting says NetEase stopped funding in May 2026 against an estimate that finishing the game needed at least ¥7 billion more, around $44 million. A publisher pulling back from overseas bets does not sign that for an unproven first title.

What I keep thinking about is the offer NetEase is said to have made instead: the studio could go independent and continue, provided it covered the cost of keeping the existing development assets and the IP.

Buy back your own work, in other words. And "assets" there means far more than what shows up in a trailer. Source meshes before decimation. UDIM texture sets. Rigs with their correctives. Cleaned mocap takes. Shader graphs, lookdev scenes, level blockouts with their revision history. And the tooling — importers, naming conventions enforced in code, a build pipeline somebody spent a year making boring.

GANG OF DRAGON was also unlucky in a specific way. It was set in Kabukicho, a real district a few streets across, built at the density you commit to when the player walks the same corner two hundred times. Assets like that are worth enormous amounts to the team continuing that game and close to nothing to anyone else. You cannot take a Kabukicho block set into a sci-fi project. You can barely take it into a different Tokyo.

Which makes the price both hard to argue down and hard to justify paying.

Neither NetEase nor Nagoshi has formally cancelled the game. The Steam page still says the date is to be announced.
