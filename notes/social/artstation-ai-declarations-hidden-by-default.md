## LinkedIn

ArtStation now asks you to declare generative AI use when you publish a project:
primarily generative AI, mixed, or 100% non-generative. On the viewer's side the
browse filter ships with declared AI work hidden by default, and the separate
"suspected AI" control set to strict, fed by user reports.

Offering "mixed" as a first-class option was the right call. A yes-or-no question
would have made liars out of everyone doing ordinary cleanup, and a label nobody
can answer truthfully is worse than no label at all.

But look at what sits in a normal 3D pipeline in 2026. The denoiser on your final
render is a neural network — OptiX and OIDN have been models for years, and
nobody thinks of hitting render as using AI. Delighting a photoscanned texture to
recover albedo is a model. Automated retopology is a model. Upscaling a 2K
texture you inherited from an old project is a model.

None of that is generative in the sense anyone means when they argue about this.
All of it is a model in the chain. A pipeline has forty steps and the form has
three buttons, and nothing published so far says where the line falls.

So "mixed" ends up absorbing everything from a denoise pass to a generated
concept somebody traced over — the honest answer for a lot of working artists,
and close to no information for whoever reads it.

Then the incentive. Declared generative work is hidden by default, and viewers
separately choose whether to let mixed through, which makes a declaration a
decision about your reach. A label that costs you views is a label people will
fudge. What would make honesty cheap is a published taxonomy: this counts, this
does not. It has not arrived.

If your portfolio is there, check what your projects now say about themselves,
and check your own filter settings. A strict suspicion default will hide some
human work from people who went looking for it.
