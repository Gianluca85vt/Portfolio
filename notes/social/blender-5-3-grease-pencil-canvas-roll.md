## LinkedIn

Ask any 2D animator what they do when a line goes wrong and they will tell you
they turn the paper. Forty-five degrees, sometimes all the way over. A wrist
draws one confident arc and one only, and an arm that reads as broken at one
angle reads as fine at another. Every application built for illustrators has had
canvas rotation for two decades.

Blender gave Grease Pencil a 3D viewport instead — orbit, pan, zoom, the
controls of a camera operator. Step inside a camera view, which is where most
finished 2D work in Blender ends up, and you were locked square to the frame.

Blender 5.3, in beta until 4 November with release expected on 10 November,
changes that. The view can be rolled inside a camera, there is an operator to
reset the roll, and the view can be flipped. Blender's developer notes list them
as three separate patches, which tells you they were three separate problems
rather than one tidy feature.

The flip is the one I would reach for most, and not for comfort. Mirroring a
canvas is how illustrators audit their own drawing: proportion and balance
errors your eye has quietly stopped registering jump out the moment the image
reads the other way round. Traditional animators did it with a light box.
Getting there in Blender used to mean mirroring the object or the camera, so you
changed the scene in order to check the drawing.

Draw Mode also picks up lasso, box, circle and pick selection, a Stroke menu
with copy, paste and delete, and the transform operators. Worth reading the
small print: selection there works on whole strokes only. Draw a line, grab it,
move it, scale it, carry on — fine. Pull a single point or adjust a tangent and
you are back in Edit Mode.

So the coverage calling this the end of mode-switching is running ahead of the
release notes. It is still the most useful thing to land in Grease Pencil in a
while, and it follows the pattern of this whole release: things artists bolted on
as add-ons becoming native, where they survive the next version.

## X

Blender 5.3 lets you roll the Grease Pencil canvas inside a camera view. And
flip it. Twenty years after every drawing app shipped the same thing, the
gesture 2D animators actually use is in the viewport.

Blender's developer notes list three separate patches: roll inside a camera,
an operator to reset the roll, and a view flip. Three problems solved
separately, not one feature shipped whole.

The flip is the one that earns its keep. Mirroring a canvas is a diagnostic —
proportion errors your eye stopped seeing become obvious when the image reads
the other way round. Animators did it with a light box.

Doing that in Blender before meant mirroring the object, or the camera, or a
flipped render in a second window. You changed the scene to check the drawing.
A view flip changes only what you look at.

Draw Mode also gains lasso, box, circle and pick selection, a Stroke menu with
copy/paste/delete, and transforms. Small print: whole strokes only. Tangents
and single points still mean Edit Mode.

So "5.3 ends mode-switching" overstates it. What 5.3 does is make the add-ons
native — canvas roll and LoopTools both — and native features survive the next
version. Beta to 4 Nov, release expected 10 Nov.
