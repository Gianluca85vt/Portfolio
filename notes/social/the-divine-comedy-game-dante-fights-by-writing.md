## LinkedIn

I'm making a game out of Dante's Divine Comedy, and in it Dante fights with a quill.
Every attack is a verse he writes in his Book, and the letters tear off the page and strike.

It's called The Divine Comedy: a souls-like for PC, built in Unreal Engine 5.8, that follows Dante from the dark wood down into Hell. The first devlog is out, and I wrote up what's in it on my blog.

Combos are lines of poetry. Spells cost Inspiration, and landed hits give it back, so the game pays you for staying in the fight and writing. The spells carry names from the poem: Fiamma antica, the old flame Dante feels when he sees Beatrice again, and Veltro, the greyhound of the first canto.

Around that sits a familiar core. Stamina, parries, deliberate fights, and Souls you lose when you die and have to recover. The new thing is what comes out of Dante's hand, so the dodge works the way a souls-like player expects it to.

The devlog is a before and after. The prototype was grey mannequins in a fog test and a Minos with a tail made of spheres. The vertical slice is the dark wood, the Gate of Hell in a dead tree with the inscription hanging in the air, the damned marching along a spine of rock, and Minos himself as the boss, half man and half serpent.

As an environment artist, the change I'd point to is the lighting. The prototype is flat and evenly grey. The slice keeps the forest cold, puts warm light under the gate, and makes the inscription the brightest thing on screen, so you read the words first.

Inferno comes first. Purgatorio and Paradiso come after, because the game follows the whole poem.
