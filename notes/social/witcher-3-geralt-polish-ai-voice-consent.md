CD Projekt Red has explained how Geralt speaks Polish in Songs of the Past, and the order of operations is the part worth reading twice.

Jacek Rozenek has voiced the Polish Geralt since 2007. He had a stroke in 2019, and the voice that came back is not the one on The Witcher 3 master. So for the expansion he recorded the new lines himself — every one of them — and a generative model shifted those recordings back toward his own performance in the base game. The work is reported as going through Respeecher, the Ukrainian firm CD Projekt used on Phantom Liberty in 2023.

Anyone who has retargeted a skeleton recognises the shape. The curves come off a human being, and what they drive is a different surface. You are not inventing motion; you are moving an existing performance onto a new target and arguing about how much the target distorts it. Here the performance is a man in a booth in 2026 and the target is the same man in 2015.

That ordering is what makes it defensible, and it is also the step a studio in a hurry would skip. Generate from text, and authorship goes with it. Record first, and there is a clean human take on disk if the conversion is ever rejected.

My one reservation is the training data. A model built from the 2014 sessions learned Rozenek and it learned the booth, the microphone and a decade-old processing chain. Convert a cleaner 2026 recording toward that reference and some of what you are pulling it toward is 2014's signal path rather than 2014's larynx. Whether it shows, nobody outside the project can say from a press line.

The transferable part of this story is not the technique. CD Projekt says its contracts require a performer's consent for AI work on their voice. That clause is checkable. Good intentions are not.
