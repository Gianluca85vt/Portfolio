## LinkedIn

In 2020, EA producer Jim Vessella went looking for the source footage behind
Command & Conquer's live-action cutscenes and came back without it. The
Remastered Collection shipped those scenes upscaled from low-quality archival
copies, frame-interpolated to 30fps. The unit art got better treatment — Lemon
Sky Studios redrew it instead of enlarging it — but the video was the best
anyone could do from what survived.

Six years on, the same producer has been digitising EA's whole video archive,
and this time the tapes were there. Around six hundred of them, per the
reporting out of the Portland Retro Gaming Expo, enough to build a Wing
Commander III remaster around. EA hasn't announced anything; what exists is a
slide from Vessella's talk listing source and upscaled versions of the FMVs,
60fps and controller support.

Why this matters to anyone who works with footage: Wing Commander III shipped
more than two hours of live action compressed to 320x240 in Origin's own
Xanmovie codec, because that is what fit on the discs in December 1994. An
upscaler fed that video has to invent the fabric weave and the edge of a
uniform, because none of it survived the encode. A master has that detail
recorded. You transfer it once and you own it.

The brake on the enthusiasm: those tapes are standard definition. The ceiling
is clean 480-line footage, not HD, and any marketing that implies otherwise is
selling an upscale with better input. Shipping the straight transfer alongside
the upscale, as the slide suggests, is the honest version.

And someone still has to play the tapes. Professional video formats from 1994
are kept alive by a handful of specialists and a smaller number of working
transports, with spares cannibalised from dead machines. The digitisation is
the real work here. The remaster is a by-product of catching it in time.
