## LinkedIn

EA borrowed roughly twenty billion dollars to buy itself in August. Last Wednesday it closed BioWare and cancelled the next Mass Effect.

Four hundred jobs went across Electronic Arts that day. About fifty of them were at BioWare, which is how little headcount was left to clear after a decade of trimming. The Mass Effect trademark did not move. It is still an asset, still carried, still worth whatever the next slide says.

Meanwhile a Stockholm trustee is taking bids on Samson until the end of this week, after Liquid Swords went bankrupt on a petition filed by its own unpaid staff. And Getty Images filed Chapter 11 in Delaware on 21 October, clearing half a billion in debt and going private under the Getty family and Koch.

Three collapses, three clean inventories. Not one of them has a column for the people.

The part that stays with me is Samson, because building environments is what I do all day. Reviewers who hated almost everything about Samson kept praising the same things: layered graffiti, dressed storefronts, reflections in wet road. The city shipped. The game around it did not. So what is being auctioned is a finished city with nobody left in the building who knows why the street bends where it bends.

That knowledge was never in the files. It was in about thirty people, and they have gone to find other work.

The Save Mass Effect petition has passed 187,000 signatures and it does not ask for BioWare back. It asks EA to keep the series going, or failing that, to license the IP to someone else. I understand the instinct. Read it as a document, though, and it is 187,000 people agreeing that the file is the thing worth saving.

Seven hundred million a year in cost cuts cannot come out of a trademark. Trademarks have no salaries.

An industry that keeps buying the street and firing the person who knew why it bends will end up with a great many streets and nobody left who can bend one.

## X

Three collapses in eleven days: BioWare closed, Liquid Swords bankrupt with a trustee taking bids on its game, Getty Images in Chapter 11. Every one produced a clean asset list. Not one of those lists has a column for the people who made the asset.

EA was taken private for $55bn in August, roughly $20bn of it borrowed, and then told investors it would find $700m a year in costs. Seven hundred million cannot come out of a trademark. Trademarks don't have salaries. Fifty jobs at BioWare do.

Samson is the one that gets me. 28 reviews, mostly negative, and the praise kept landing on the same things: layered graffiti, dressed storefronts, reflections in wet road. The environment art shipped. The missions it was built to carry didn't.

A city is a few thousand undocumented decisions. The street that bends to close a sightline. The window left blank because the interior got cut in week nine. A kerb raised 6cm so the car stops scraping. None of that is in the files. It was in 30 people.

Compare Wildwood: 131 minutes, ~188,000 exposures, somewhere between 1,830 and 2,620 animator-weeks at Laika's own stated rates. Third at the box office on a reported $100m. No trustee can auction the capability, because the capability is the hands.

Keep buying the street and firing the person who knew why it bends, and you end up with a great many streets and nobody left who can bend one.
