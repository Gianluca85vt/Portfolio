## LinkedIn

Jensen Huang resigned from Nvidia on 12 November, and I want to make an
unpopular argument about what it means for those of us who build render boxes
for a living: almost nothing, in the direction everyone is looking.

Colette Kress, CFO since 2013, is interim chief executive while keeping the CFO
job. Harvey C. Jones becomes the company's first separate board chair. The board
has opened a search that may go outside the company.

Here is why the roadmap is safe. The GPU you will buy in eighteen months was
architecturally frozen years ago, taped out months ago, and its fab capacity was
booked and paid for long before this week. Nobody arriving in that office on a
Thursday makes a different chip appear in 2027. And the reason a workstation is
an Nvidia workstation has very little to do with who runs Nvidia: CUDA is twenty
years old, OptiX sits on it, and so do Redshift, Octane, Arnold's GPU renderer
and the Cycles backend most Blender users actually render with. Thousands of
engineer-years of driver work. A new chief executive inherits all of it.

The decision I would actually watch is allocation — how much industrial capacity
goes to data-centre parts versus GeForce. That is the decision behind a 5090
that launched at $1,999 and was asking $4,929 on Nvidia's own store in August,
and behind October's reports that the GB202 die is being reserved for
professional cards, which would take the top GeForce from 32GB of VRAM down to
24. A heavy scene fits in memory or it does not. There is no graceful
degradation at that line.

An interim leader who is also the CFO, with a results call days away, is not the
configuration I would bet on to move wafers toward the lower-margin product
line. That is inference, and I would be glad to be wrong.

We will know in February, when a card has a price on it.
