## LinkedIn

Valve has told developers that checking a build before release can now take up
to 12 business days, against the three to five it used to quote. For a
four-person studio that is the difference between a launch and a missed one.

The cases from the last fortnight are specific. Studio Daimon submitted the
Early Access build of Food Devils on 12 October and was still waiting when its
launch date came round. 2 Left Thumbs said Wheel Make It slipped by nearly two
weeks and then opened to 15 concurrent players against more than 11,000
wishlists. Caravan Carnage: Fruity Road went in on 16 October and was approved
on 1 November — sixteen calendar days.

The re-review is the expensive part. Valve's stated reason for the backlog is
a jump in submissions plus builds it has to check more than once, and a build
that fails review on a configuration error costs twelve days, an afternoon's
fix, and a fresh place at the back of the line.

Which is why the new Steamworks checklist reads like a bug report on what has
been arriving. Five minutes of recorded Steam playtime on the current build
before you submit. Declare your redistributables. Have someone in the partner
group write a save to Steam Cloud and confirm it comes back. Clean up
achievements with missing icons or duplicate entries.

Every one works perfectly on the machine the game was built on. Launch options
point at the wrong binary and nobody notices until a stranger presses Play. A
depot assigned to the wrong branch ships a game with no audio or no second
level, which from a reviewer's chair is a broken game.

Practical version for anyone shipping on Steam in the next quarter: budget
three weeks of slack, treat the submission build as a cold-start test on a
machine that has never seen your project, and announce the date after approval
lands rather than before. The last one is expensive advice. It is cheaper than
11,000 wishlists firing into an empty room.
