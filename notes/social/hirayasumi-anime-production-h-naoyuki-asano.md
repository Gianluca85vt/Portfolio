## LinkedIn

Anime News Network argued yesterday that Keigo Shinzō's Hirayasumi will "translate perfectly to animation" — the soft line work, the rounded designs, the watercolour backgrounds. I want to take that claim seriously, because it describes the exact place adaptations of this kind fall over.

Shinzō's line thickens and thins. Corners go soft. Shapes often do not fully close. That is most of the charm, and it is also, item by item, every property a television animation pipeline exists to remove. A broadcast design has to be drawable by key animators, in-between artists and second-tier studios taking a slice of the episode count, and the standard way to make a design survive that many hands is to flatten it: uniform line weight, fewer features, closed shapes — because the digital paint step fills regions with a bucket, and a line with a gap in it leaks colour across the cel.

So you either keep the wobble and pay in correction passes and episode-to-episode drift, or you clean it up and ship a perfectly nice show that looks like eleven others.

The backgrounds are the harder half. Watercolour behaves badly in motion: paper grain is high-frequency detail, and high-frequency detail aliases first under a camera move, crawls first under scaling, and is the first thing an encoder discards. Bake it into the plate and it slides like a sticker during a parallax move. Generate it per frame and it boils. And the watercolour being praised is cover art — one image, painted once. A season needs hundreds of plates on a fixed calendar.

Which is why the staff list is the interesting document here. Naoyuki Asano, who designed Keep Your Hands Off Eizouken! at Science SARU, has the character designs — somebody who has shipped a show built on characters allowed to deform knows in episodes, not in theory, how much looseness a schedule can carry. The studio is Production +h., whose Dead Dead Demons Dededede Destruction meant solving the same class of problem for Inio Asano. Mika Nishimura is art director. Kei Suezawa directs, writes and handles series composition.

Nobody in that list is a generalist hire. When the drawing is the point, you staff the drawing first.

NHK General, January 2027.
