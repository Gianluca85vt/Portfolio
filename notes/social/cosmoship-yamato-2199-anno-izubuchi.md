Production I.G and Khara opened the site for Cosmoship Yamato √2199 this week. A title, a staff list, one teaser visual, and a credit that tells you more about the pipeline than the visual does.

Hideaki Anno directs. Yutaka Izubuchi co-directs. Both of them are also credited on design works.

On a film whose protagonist is a ship, that matters. A Yamato hull is one of the nastiest assets in the genre — hundreds of metres of panel line, and a franchise whose signature shot is a slow crawl along its length. Cel-shaded hard surface at that scale shows you exactly where the normals break and where the line weight thins out.

Normally that asset passes through three sets of hands. Mech design draws the sheet. A modelling team builds it, usually at another company. Layout frames whatever comes back. Every handoff is a chance for the model to stop reading like the drawing, and the director only sees the result once all three have happened.

Putting direction and design works in the same two pairs of hands removes one of those gaps. Whether it produces a better ship, I genuinely don't know. It does mean nobody has to send a note downstream asking why the bow looks wrong.

Izubuchi solved a version of this before, on 2199 in 2012: CG took the monitor graphics, the props, the strategy board and the engine schematics, character work stayed on paper, and the integration was tuned so the seam didn't show. That discipline came partly from a weekly television schedule. A feature gets graded differently and watched from closer.

The open question is whose house look governs the hull. I.G holds its CG fleets as CG. The Khara films flatten geometry until you stop reading it as geometry. Both work. They produce different ships, and nothing announced says which studio owns the asset.
