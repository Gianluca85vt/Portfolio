Sega released a thirteen-minute hand-drawn Sonic short on YouTube yesterday, free, for the franchise's thirty-fifth anniversary. Try to find out which studio animated it.

The director is in every write-up: Kevin Molina-Ortiz, who came up on Rise of the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles. Ian Flynn wrote it. Takashi Iizuka of Sonic Team gets the story credit. All three names are consistent across the coverage.

The animation studio is not. One account has it produced by Studio GIGGEX and animated by SIMAGE Animations. Others print GXS Productions. When the short was announced at Anime Expo in July, The Sonic Stadium read the panel and concluded it was probably Giggex — which is a reporter inferring, because the press material did not say.

Thirteen minutes of 2D is layout, key animation, in-betweens, clean-up, colour, backgrounds, comp and a mix. Months of work, dozens of artists, most of them on contracts that end when the short does. The brand gets the title card. The director gets the press release. The house that put pencils on it gets a line in a credit roll nobody transcribes.

That credit is the only portable thing most of those artists leave with. It is how a 2D studio wins its next commission and how an individual in-betweener points at something and says that was us.

Worth saying the other half too: this is a good job. A thirteen-minute piece on a famous property with no broadcaster squeezing the runtime is a brief 2D crews rarely get. Which is the argument for naming the people who took it, not against.

The thing I want to watch when I sit with it properly: Sonic has one of the most tightly policed style guides in games — quill count, eye geometry, the arc of the shoes, locked for three decades of licensing. Molina-Ortiz is known for stretching characters hard through a movement. How far off model the drawing is allowed to go is the most interesting question in this production, and it is not one you could have predicted from the announcement.
