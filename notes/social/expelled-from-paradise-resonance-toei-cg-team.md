## LinkedIn

The sequel to Expelled from Paradise opened in Japan on 13 November and placed fifth on its first weekend. Seiji Mizushima directed again, Gen Urobuchi wrote it again, the character designer and composer came back. One name did not: Graphinica, who animated the 2014 original and made it the film people pointed at when they wanted to argue that full-3DCG anime could look good.

Toei Animation took the work in-house instead. Per reporting by Tokyo Anime Dispatch, they started a new CG team in early 2023 — a label called noise animation label — to make this one picture.

The detail that got me: Toei's Oizumi studio already ran a pipeline built on Maya. Most of the artists they gathered preferred 3ds Max. So the team rebuilt the pipeline from scratch.

Anyone who has taken a show through a DCC migration knows what that sentence costs. The software is the smallest part of a pipeline. The rest is the asset naming, the publish and load steps, the rigs and the scripts that drive them, render submission, the shader library, and the tools one person wrote in a week that forty people now depend on. None of it ports. You rewrite it while the film is in production, which is the worst possible time to be debugging your own plumbing.

Toei chose the artists over the plumbing. For a studio with their volume that is an expensive call, and I think it is the right one, because the thing you cannot rebuild in eighteen months is a crew's muscle memory.

The reported technique fits: animation posed by hand inside the CG, almost nothing drawn on top except elements like smoke. Keep the model honest, let the renderer carry the stylisation. ANN's reviewer gave the film a B- overall and a B- for animation, and called the shading drastically better than 2014.

Sounds right. Lookdev scales — solve a skin shader once and it serves the whole cast. Character acting does not, because the only cure for a flat performance is an animator spending more days on it.
