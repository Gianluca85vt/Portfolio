## LinkedIn

ScreenRant scored Stupid Never Dies 4 out of 10 this week, and then broke that
number into sub-scores: Visuals 9, Gameplay 4.

That single review contains the whole story of the game's reception. Across the
twenty-one verdicts I could verify and attribute, the mean is 7.2 and the range
runs from 4 to 9 — five points wide on a ten-point scale, which is the widest
split I have logged on a release this size. OpenCritic sits at 70 from 43
critics, Metacritic at 66 from 28, both as of 22 October.

The interesting part for anyone who makes this stuff is where the agreement
stops. Reviewers are close to unanimous on the art direction. They scatter
across five points on how it plays. Art direction is legible from a still;
combat feel is legible from nothing except a controller. So a spread like this
is not reviewers disagreeing about the game, it is them reporting their own
tolerance for missed input.

The complaints triangulate into one systems problem rather than four separate
ones. No lock-on. Healing that requires biting a specific enemy, so a heal is
gated behind a precise melee connection. Aerial enemies as the recurring
frustration. A timer degrading the protagonist over the run. Each of those is
defensible alone. Stacked, they are where the 4s come from.

What makes it surprising is the lineage. GPTRACK50 was founded by Hiroyuki
Kobayashi, who produced Devil May Cry, Dragon's Dogma and Resident Evil 4 at
Capcom, and the team around him is largely Capcom action people. Targeting and
camera is the department you would expect them to have solved before anything
else. Sega co-publishes; it shipped 21 October on PS5 and PC.

Two weeks ago Hellraiser Revival split four points wide on the same axis —
everyone liked the Labyrinth, nobody agreed about the fighting in it. Two games,
two weeks, one fracture line.
