## LinkedIn

Sega pulled Total War: Shogun 2 – Complete Edition from sale two days after
launch and put the 2011 original back up. The statement from Sega and Creative
Assembly says the release fell short of expectations and of their own
standards. It does not mention AI, which is what most of the Steam reviews
were about.

Worth separating two complaints that have been collapsed into one.

The Shogun 2 unit cards were painted small in 2011 — a few hundred portraits
you read in half a second while hovering a stack. In the Complete Edition they
came back soft and shiny. That is what an enlarger does to a small painting:
it has no new information, so it manufactures smooth gradients where the
brushwork used to be. Ugly, cheap, explicable.

Then there are the screenshots with distorted faces, a helmet melting into the
face under it, a hand with a sixth finger. An upscaler does not grow a digit.
It does not know the thing is a hand. Anatomy appearing is generation, not
enlargement — and at least one outlet covering this made the same distinction.
Across several hundred cards both things can be true at once. Nobody outside
the studio can say which tool touched which asset, and there is no
generative-AI disclosure on the store page.

The production reason underneath it is the one I keep running into. Source art
for a 2011 game is often gone — migrated off a server, left on a contractor's
drive, or never layered because the card was painted flat and exported once.
So the bin holds final 256-pixel PNGs and the options are: commission a
repaint, ship the originals untouched, or run them through something.

EA paid Lemon Sky to redraw Command & Conquer's unit art by hand when the
masters could not be found. That cost money. It is also why nobody argues
about those assets fifteen years later.

Shipping the 2011 cards as they were would have passed. That option is free,
and it keeps getting skipped.
