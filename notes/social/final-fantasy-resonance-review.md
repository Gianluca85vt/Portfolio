Nine reviews are in for Final Fantasy Resonance and they run from 6 to 10. A four-point spread is wide, and the useful thing about a wide spread is that it tells you where the argument is.

It is not the art. Across the whole range, from Famitsu's four 9s to the 3-out-of-5s at Eurogamer and VGC, the visual work gets the praise. The complaint is the writing, and it turns up in the 8s as often as the 6s.

That is worth sitting with, because of where the writing came from. Resonance adapts the first story arc of Final Fantasy Brave Exvius — the mobile gacha — into a standalone turn-based RPG. Square Enix publishes, Lancarse co-develops. It is also the first Final Fantasy built in the HD-2D style.

Square Enix calls it a rebuild rather than a port, and as an asset claim that holds up. Gacha character art is portrait-first: the budget goes into one splash illustration that reads at a couple of hundred pixels in a summon screen, plus a combat sprite that mostly holds a pose. HD-2D wants the opposite distribution — full-body sprites with walk cycles, idles, casts and hit reactions, sitting on dioramas built in depth layers so the tilt-shift and the light shafts have something to separate. You cannot scale the first into the second. You redraw it, and from the footage somebody did.

What a rewrite cannot easily fix is shape. A gacha arc is delivered in episodes over months, between events, each beat written to land in a short session. Compress it into 25 hours bought in one go and the rhythm it was designed for stops helping. That is a structural inheritance, not a prose problem, and the reviews read like it.

Worth knowing if you are deciding: the free demo is the whole of chapter one, three to five hours, and the save carries over. For a €49.99 release that is a confident offer.
