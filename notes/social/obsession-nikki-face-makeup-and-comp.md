For six months the record on Obsession said that Nikki's face — the one that makes you uneasy without letting you say which part moved — was a practical effect. Reviewers credited the makeup chair. One called the film a masterclass in subtlety and left it there.

A befores & afters interview published today fills in the other half. Production VFX supervisor Peter Timberlake and his studio, Queen, built the augmentation as practical make-up plus a 2D compositing pass, using an in-house toolset they call Plastik, aimed at a deliberate uncanny valley. Subtle shifts to the actor's features, nothing more.

The design itself came off a phone. Bloody Disgusting reports the look was drawn from an uncanny-valley trick popularised on TikTok by a beauty creator around 2023, with key makeup artist Kate Oja sharing the images. Amateurs on a vertical video worked out which small deviations in a human face unsettle a viewer, and a studio horror film used the answer.

The part I keep thinking about is why the top-up stayed in 2D. The obvious route for "make this actress subtly inhuman" is to scan her, rebuild the head, solve the performance onto it and render. Total control, every millimetre a parameter. It also throws the take away — once the head is rebuilt, the asymmetric micro-expressions and the small timing offsets have to be recovered through a solve, and solves lose exactly those things. On a film where the lead performance is the engine, that is a bad trade.

A tracked 2D warp keeps her on screen. The work moves to tracking skin that deforms, warping without tearing frame over frame, and matching grain and falloff at the edge — which is where invisible work usually stops being invisible.

Do it well and nobody can point at it. Which is why the makeup got the credit and the comp got none.

The obvious alternative was a 3D route: scan her, rebuild the head, solve the performance on. Total control, every millimetre a parameter.

So the cost moves into comp: tracking skin that deforms with no rigid transform to lock to, warping without tearing, matching grain and falloff at the edge of the warp.
