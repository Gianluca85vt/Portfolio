Kenta Shinohara ended Witch Watch this week at chapter 270, after five years in Weekly Shonen Jump. The anime, though, is only twenty-five episodes in — and season 2 is dated 2027.

That order of events is worth paying attention to if you make things for a living.

Season 1 at Bibury Animation Studios was built the way most long-running adaptations are: against a serial that was still running. Nobody involved knew how it ended. Model sheets get locked early, and if the author later draws a character's final form or promotes a throwaway location into something load-bearing, the production has already committed. Series composition is the harder problem — you are dividing an unknown number of chapters into a known number of episodes, so you pace conservatively and stop somewhere that is not an ending.

Season 2 gets none of that uncertainty. The composition writer can count the remaining chapters, divide, and put the expensive sequences where the story actually peaks rather than where the schedule guessed. Designs get drawn once, against their final state. Nobody has to invent an anime-original ending.

The other thing I keep coming back to is how expensive this particular show is to make. An action series is a reuse machine — design a character, a weapon, a few locations, amortise across fifty episodes. A gag comedy inverts that. Witch Watch runs on transformations and one-off visual jokes, and every one needs its own layout, often its own background, sometimes a design that lives for eleven seconds and never returns. None of it amortises. You pay full price every week.

Which is why a 2027 date reads to me as sensible rather than slow. A comedy that cannot afford its gags stops being a comedy.

What I am watching for: whether Bibury is still on it, and whether season 2 comes back as one cour or two. Roughly 170 to 190 chapters are still unadapted. That is three or four cours of television, not one.
