## LinkedIn

Rakuten Viki's press release for The Painted Soul contains one carefully built sentence, and if you work in production it is the only sentence that matters.

The concept and storytelling, it says, were developed by a team of creatives at Imaginus "who utilized AI and other technologies."

Read that again. Creatives developed it. AI was among the tools. And the work being described is concept and storytelling — pre-production. Not animation, not design, not compositing.

The ten-episode series premieres on Viki on 21 October 2026. Imaginus is the studio Jinnie Choi founded in December 2021 after five years as Studio Dragon's inaugural CEO, so this is not somebody's first production. Choi Junbae directs, off Mouse and Stealer: The Treasure Keeper — both live action, which is its own interesting decision. In live action a reaction shot costs a camera setup. In animation it costs a drawing, and a director who does not feel that in their hands writes coverage nobody can afford to draw.

What the release never says is how the ten episodes were made. No technique, no production breakdown, no partner studio. For a live-action drama that silence would be ordinary. For an animated series the technique is the show — 2D on threes reads nothing like cel-shaded 3D, and a viewer clocks the difference in four seconds even if they could not name it. Headlines have already flattened the whole thing into "AI-assisted Korean-animated series," which claims a good deal more than the release does.

Here is the pattern I keep finding. Two days before the premiere, Lezhin Entertainment launched Teaz — AI character chat built on official webtoon IP from Lezhin Comics and Bomtoon. Progress through it unlocks hidden illustrations drawn by the original creators.

Same week, same industry. AI does the concept pass at one company and the conversation at the other. At both, the drawn image is the thing held back as the reward.

The model generates the approach. The hand is the payoff.
