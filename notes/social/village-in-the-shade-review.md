## LinkedIn

Nippon Ichi shipped Village in the Shade with a switch in the options menu.
Shade Mode is the whole game: a village, a farm, a curfew, and nights you are
supposed to be afraid of. Sunshine Mode removes the horror and leaves the
farming. Same village, same neighbours, same crops.

That is a harder art brief than it looks. Shipping the dread as a mode means
the village has to survive being read twice, and the daytime read is the
expensive one. If the place only worked because the light was wrong, Sunshine
Mode falls apart and takes the review scores with it. Reviewers who tried it
describe a farm sim that still functions on its own merits, which is a quiet
compliment to whoever laid out that village.

The thing that makes it affordable is the house style. Nippon Ichi has never
bought fidelity — Disgaea, Yomawari, the Labyrinth games all got their look
from committing hard to a style and keeping polygon and texture budgets
somewhere a small team can finish them. Flat-shaded, stylised geometry takes a
colour temperature shift cleanly. Scanned, physically based assets fight you
on it, and you end up authoring a second set of night materials nobody
budgeted for. RPGFan gave the graphics a hundred out of a hundred, on a game
that is not technically expensive anywhere.

Where it slips is performance, and it slips in the wrong room. Reviewers on
Switch 2 put the frame rate between 45 and 60 in the main town, holding out on
the farm. Predictable — the town is where NPCs, shadow casters and one-off
props land in the same draw call budget. Also the worst place for it in this
genre, because a farm sim walks you through the hub four or five times a day.

Ten verdicts I could verify average 8.5, from 7.9 to 9.5. No dissent, only
degrees of enthusiasm, plus one real argument about the opening hours.
