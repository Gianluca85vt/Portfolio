## LinkedIn

Micron has taken the 4F² DRAM cell off its roadmap after roughly twenty years of work, and its CTO said the quiet part out loud: "DRAM is nearing atomic limits at this point."

If you build 3D scenes for a living, that sentence matters more than any price chart.

Here is the background. A DRAM cell's area is measured in F², where F is the smallest feature the process can print. The conventional cell is 6F². The industry has spent most of two decades chasing 4F², which would cut the area of every stored bit by a third — the biggest density win available without rebuilding the architecture from scratch. Micron has now concluded that the vertical-channel transistor it requires costs more in process complexity and yield than the area it gives back. Samsung, SK hynix and CXMT are reportedly still pursuing it. One of those positions is wrong, and we will find out which in about four years.

What Micron is doing instead, from its 21 October technology webinar: 1γ is in high-volume manufacturing now; 1δ enters volume production in the second half of 2027 with a bit-density gain in the mid-20% range; 1ε introduces wafer bonding, with no detail disclosed; High-NA EUV and new materials come after that. 3D DRAM appears on the slide as 3D1 and 3D2 with no architecture and no dates.

Every ceiling I have hit in a scene was a memory ceiling. Not polygon count — that stopped being the problem years ago. It is how many 4K textures stay resident before the viewport starts swapping, and whether a sim caches to RAM or to disk and costs you the afternoon.

For fifteen years that number went up on its own and nobody had to plan for it. Bits come from area now, area comes from wafers, and wafers are being bought by people with far deeper pockets than anyone rendering for a living.

A mid-20% gain in late 2027, aimed mostly at HBM, is not relief. It is a plateau with good engineering on it.

That sentence is the supply side of every RAM price story this year.

Samsung, SK hynix and CXMT are reportedly still pursuing 4F².

So the handful of companies that make all the world's DRAM now disagree about the next cell architecture. One side is wrong, and it is an expensive thing to be wrong about.

The roadmap instead, from Micron's 21 Oct webinar:

1γ — in HVM now
1δ — volume H2 2027, mid-20% density gain
1ε — wafer bonding, no detail given
ζÅ — High-NA EUV
ηÅ — new materials

3D DRAM sits on the slide as 3D1/3D2. No architecture, no dates.

Wafer bonding is the interesting move: build the cell array and the peripheral logic on separate wafers at processes that suit each, then bond them face to face.

NAND has done a version of this for years. It is a packaging answer to a lithography problem.

Why #b3d and VFX people should care: every scene ceiling is a memory ceiling. Texture residency, cache warmth, whether a sim fits in RAM or dies to disk.

That number used to climb on its own. Micron just said it does not any more.
