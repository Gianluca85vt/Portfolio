The PlayStation 6's memory and storage alone reportedly cost more than $680 a console. That's a leaker's figure rather than Sony's, but it's the one VGC and Eurogamer both ran this weekend.

Same few days: leaks say Nvidia has stopped making the RTX 5090 so its die can go to professional and data centre cards. If that holds, 24GB becomes the most memory you can buy on a GeForce.

One allocation argument, two invoices, and both land on my desk.

Memory is the one number on a spec sheet I actually worry about. Almost everything else is a dial. Fewer shadow casters, a cheaper reflection method, drop a bounce — turn the dial and the frame gets cheaper and uglier by a percentage, and most of the job is knowing which percentages people forgive.

Memory doesn't work like that. The scene fits on the card or it doesn't.

On the wrong side of that line you're not 15% slower. You're crawling across PCIe for every evicted texture fetch, or the render is dead and your night went with it. So 32GB down to 24GB costs more than eight gigabytes. It costs a 4K set on your hero assets, or the sim cache for a medium effect, or the headroom to keep working in the viewport while something renders behind you.

What gets me is who decided it. For thirty years consoles were how memory got cheap — a generation bought DRAM by the tens of millions of units and pulled the price curve down for everyone, artists included. TrendForce reckons AI will take close to a fifth of global DRAM wafer capacity this year. IDC sees no relief before the end of 2027. A console maker negotiating for 30GB a unit is a rounding error beside a data centre order now.

The hardest constraint in our entire pipeline, the one that decides how big a level can be and how many unique materials it carries, got settled in a negotiation where nobody who makes games had a seat.

Go over and you're not 15% slower. You're crawling across PCIe for every evicted texture fetch, or the render dies overnight. Eviction hits the data you touch most, so a gigabyte over can cost you a huge fraction of your speed.

Which is why 32GB to 24GB costs more than 8GB. That headroom is a 4K set on your hero assets, or a medium sim cache, or the viewport staying usable while something renders behind you. On 32 it isn't a decision. On 24 it is.
