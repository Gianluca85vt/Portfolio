A restroom sign in Marvel's Wolverine got past 700,000 views this weekend as evidence the game used generative AI. It was a UV map bug. Overlapping islands in a texture layout, the most ordinary error in environment art since the nineties.

Eight days earlier somebody reviewed an indie game called Luminary after 24 minutes and said the AI voice ruined it. The voice was the creative director's wife. First time in a booth.

Same week, CESA put generative AI use among Japanese developers at 85.8%, up from 51%. Producing visuals sits sixth on the task list at 16%, under paperwork, code assistance and localisation. Most of those people are using it on admin.

The headline carries none of that, and the headline is what players read.

Photography is building hardware against this. There is a camera that cryptographically signs its footage at capture, and it needs a 40mm fan inside the shell so the chip doesn't throttle doing the maths.

We have a disclosure checkbox the publisher ticks about itself, and Wolverine is a PS5 exclusive so it doesn't even have that. The proof offered to 700,000 people was a community director typing two lines on a Sunday.

Doubt was the cheap thing to build. It got built first.
