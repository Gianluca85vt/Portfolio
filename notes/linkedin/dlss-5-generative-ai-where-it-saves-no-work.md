Rockstar put GTA 6 above 600,000 animations this week. Red Dead 2 was around 300,000. GTA 5 was around 55,000.

Thirteen years to multiply it by ten, and an office in Los Angeles that worked on nothing but pedestrians.

That is where the money goes in this job. The connective tissue. One believable sit-down is the approach, the turn, the weight shift, the settle — then the same again for a stool, then a booth, then the exits, which are not the entries played backwards. Nobody looks at any of it directly and everybody notices when it is missing.

DLSS 5 landed the same week. Its neural pass runs after the frame has been assembled, lit and graded, so everything it touches has already been paid for. It changes the result once the cost is sunk and the people who authored the look are on another project.

A generative tool pointed at that long tail — opted into per project, tuned inside the building, with an art lead accountable for what comes out — would sell itself to every mid-sized studio in Europe.

What shipped is a filter for the last millisecond.

Rockstar can staff a building instead. Most teams cannot, and nobody is building them the other thing.
