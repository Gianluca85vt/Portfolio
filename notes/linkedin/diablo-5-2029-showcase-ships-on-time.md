Blizzard dated Diablo 5 for spring 2029 and a StarCraft shooter for 2030. Its president said the games were announced now to show confidence.

At BlizzCon 2023 the company announced three WoW expansions at once. This weekend the third of them got a cinematic and more details promised next year.

A day earlier, Level-5 apologised for the generative AI in its own showcase. It wanted the presentation to be more spectacular.

That is where a machine always turns up first. A trailer is the one surface in a games company with a fixed public date, no engine, no QA pass, no certification and nobody downstream who has to open the file again. A melted window frame in a trailer breaks nothing. The same frame in the level ships for ten years.

On 11 September Polyarc closed. Twenty-nine people, two well-reviewed Moss games, a flatscreen port in July that pulled 200,000 wishlists and not enough money. The studio's last act was publishing every name, title and portfolio link so somebody else would hire them.

Blizzard gets believed about 2030. Polyarc shipped games that work and could not hold twenty-nine desks past September.

Confidence is the only thing in this industry still shipping on schedule.
