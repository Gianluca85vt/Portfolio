## 7 · Twenty billion, borrowed — 70s

**SHOT** — MS, wider than the Samson section. Giving the money its own frame size.

**GRAPHIC** — `03-the-cut.svg`. In at 00:20 of this section, bars growing from the left over 10 frames, hold 7s. The note about reported figures stays on screen the whole hold — do not trim it.

> Read the BioWare closure as a repayment schedule and it stops being mysterious.
>
> PIF, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners took EA private for fifty-five billion dollars in August. / Reporting put roughly twenty billion of that as borrowed. / EA then told investors it would find seven hundred million a year in costs.
>
> Seven hundred million a year has to come from somewhere. / It doesn't come out of a trademark. Trademarks don't have salaries. People do.
>
> Fifty jobs at BioWare is a rounding error against seven hundred million, / which is precisely why it happened. / The studio was small enough by then that closing it was cheap, tidy and visible — / a decision that reads well on a cost line and costs nothing on an asset line.
>
> Everything EA named as the destination is something it already owns. / Sports. Apex. Battlefield. The next Jedi game.
>
> Buy a company with debt, then pay the debt with the people who made it worth buying. / It works, in the narrow sense that the arithmetic closes.

**DELIVERY** — "It works" has to be read genuinely, not sarcastically. The sarcasm is in "narrow".

## 8 · A hundred and eighty-eight thousand frames — 65s

**SHOT** — Back to MCU. Warmer delivery from here; this is the section with admiration in it.

**GRAPHIC** — `06-the-frames.svg`. In at 00:18 of this section, bars growing left to right, hold 9s, fade on "my sums".

> Which brings me to the one production this week that cannot be turned into a list.
>
> Wildwood opened on Friday. A hundred and thirty-one minutes — / the longest stop-motion feature anybody has released — / which is about a hundred and eighty-eight thousand exposures at twenty-four frames a second.
>
> Laika's own stated target is around four point three seconds of finished animation per animator per week. / On Kubo, the reality came in nearer three. / Run those numbers and Wildwood sits somewhere between one thousand eight hundred and two thousand six hundred animator-weeks of shooting. / Those are my sums, not Laika's.
>
> Those are weeks on a stage, one frame at a time, / by people each finishing about three seconds of usable footage before the weekend. / Shooting weeks, not the whole production.
>
> It took third place at the box office, against a reported budget near a hundred million dollars. / In the language of the other three stories, that makes it a bad asset.

**DELIVERY** — Slow down across the arithmetic. The numbers are the admiration in this piece, so give them room rather than rattling them off.

**Pinned comment**

```
Four caveats on the figures, because several of them are reported rather
than confirmed.

The ~$20bn of borrowing and the $700m of annual cost cuts come from
reporting around the August close, not from EA. The $55bn purchase price
and the 21 October closure of BioWare are confirmed.

The animator-week numbers for Wildwood are my own arithmetic: 131 minutes
is about 188,000 exposures at 24fps, divided by Laika's publicly stated
throughput target of ~4.3 seconds per animator per week, and by the ~3
seconds Kubo reportedly averaged. They count shooting weeks, not total
production, and Laika has published nothing about Wildwood's own rate.

Samson's staff arrears are reported as either two months or "since
August", and the headcount as between 27 and about 30. Nobody has
published an exact figure.

Wildwood's runtime is listed as 131 minutes in most places and 132 in a
few. I used 131.
```
