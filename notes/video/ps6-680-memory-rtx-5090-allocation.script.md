## Two — slope and wall (68s)

**SHOT** — Slight push in, or just lean forward. The shot should tighten for this section because it is the craft part.

> Nearly everything on a specification sheet is a slope. / Fewer shadow casters. A cheaper reflection method. Drop a bounce. Let the level of detail chain start a step earlier.

> Turn any one of those dials and the frame gets cheaper by a percentage and uglier by a percentage, / and most of this job is learning which percentages a viewer forgives. Lighting is mostly a negotiation about what nobody is going to look at.

> Memory does not negotiate. / The scene is resident on the card or it is not.

> On the wrong side of that line you are not fifteen per cent slower. / You are crawling across the bus for every texture fetch that got evicted, / or the render is dead and your night has gone with it. Eviction takes the data you touch most, / so a gigabyte over the limit can cost a huge fraction of your speed.

> A memory ceiling is a wall. / The wall is where your ambition for the shot stops.

**GRAPHIC** — `05-slope-and-wall.svg`. In on "Memory does not negotiate", rows revealing top to bottom, one per sentence. Hold 12s through to "or dies". Out slowly — 0.6s dissolve, not a cut.

**DELIVERY** — The list of dials is said fast, almost thrown away. Then full stop before "Memory does not negotiate" and drop the pitch. That contrast is the whole section.

**B-ROLL** — Screen capture of a viewport struggling, or a render log with an out-of-core warning, is perfect here. If you have none, the plate carries it alone; do not mime typing.

## Three — the missing rung (72s)

**SHOT** — Cut back out to the medium from the Who I am section.

**CUT** — Deliberate, on the word "ladder".

> The ladder used to have a rung in the middle.

> Below it, a consumer card you could justify. Above it, a workstation card with three times the capacity, / for four to six times the money.

> The 5090 sat between them. It did most of what the expensive one did at a quarter of the price, / which is why every freelancer I know who renders on a GPU either owns one or wanted one.

> Take it out and the ladder is a consumer card and an invoice. / Nothing in between. The thing on the far side of that gap is priced for a company that bills the render, / not for a person.

> Look at where the card is going, though. The same silicon, / sold into a market that pays more for it. There is no villain in that. It is what any of us would do with a wafer we could sell twice.

> It does mean the hardware an artist works on is now the leftovers of a business with no interest in art.

**GRAPHIC** — `03-the-ceiling.svg`. In on "The ladder used to have a rung", bars growing in sequence, the last bar arriving a beat late and in a different weight so the drop reads. Hold 10s. The note about the leak must be legible — do not shorten the hold to make the edit tidier.

**DELIVERY** — "There is no villain in that" is said straight, not grudgingly. The argument is stronger when it concedes the obvious.

## Four — lodgers (72s)

**SHOT** — Tighten again. Same push as section two, so the two craft sections match.

> Then the software turns up with its hat in its hand.

> Nvidia's revised DLSS 5 model is around a hundred and eighteen million parameters, / down roughly a quarter. Nvidia says it costs half the overhead / and twenty per cent less memory at four K.

> Those are Nvidia's own figures, unchecked by anyone outside. / Take them anyway, because of the shape of the claim: / a graphics feature now sells on taking less of the thing that just got scarce.

> A neural pass living in the frame buffer is a lodger in a flat that was already too small. It wants its slice resident, permanently, / while the scene wants all of it. So you switch it off — / and a feature artists switch off is one the platform holder eventually makes mandatory, because the target was set assuming it was on.

> The sequence is funny. Memory got scarce because of the AI build-outs. / The fix shipped into the gap is a smaller AI model. We are sold a diet by the people who ate the food.

**DELIVERY** — The last line is the only joke in the piece. Land it dry and move on immediately; do not wait for the laugh.

**B-ROLL** — Nothing needed. If you want something, a graphics settings menu scrolling past the upscaling options works. Avoid gameplay footage here; it argues the opposite point.

## Five — nobody was bidding (66s)

**SHOT** — Wide. Pull all the way out for the last argument.

> For thirty years, consoles were how memory got cheap.

> A console generation bought memory by the tens of millions of units / and dragged the whole price curve down behind it. Every artist in this industry has been living off that subsidy / without once thinking about it. The target specification went up every five years because memory got cheaper every year, / and the reason memory got cheaper every year was partly us.

> That has reversed, and nobody asked us.

> A console maker negotiating for thirty gigabytes a unit is no longer the big customer in the room. / It is a rounding error beside a data centre order, / and it gets the price a rounding error gets.

> Which means the memory budget an art team will build against for the next decade, / the thing that decides how big a level can be and how many unique materials it can carry, / was settled in a supply negotiation where games had no seat.

**GRAPHIC** — `06-set-before-us.svg`. In on "was settled in a supply negotiation", stages drawing left to right over 2s, the mark on the first stage landing hard. Hold 8s.

**GRAPHIC** — `07-the-subsidy.svg`. Straight cut from the timeline, no dissolve, on "rounding error gets" if you reorder in the edit — otherwise bring it in under the "thirty years" paragraph and hold 10s.

**DELIVERY** — "That has reversed, and nobody asked us" sits alone. Three quarters of a second of air either side of it.
