## Cold open — 24s

**SHOT** — Medium close, straight down the lens. First frame is the first word;
no logo, no sting, no music bed.

> In 1994 somebody at Electronic Arts labelled a videotape / and put it
> somewhere dry. Thirty-two years later that tape is the reason you can buy
> Wing Commander on Steam this morning / at a quality anybody would ship.

**DELIVERY** — Flat and quick. The word "videotape" gets no emphasis at all;
the sentence does the work, and a lean on it turns this into a gimmick.

> The software side of the same company, with a hundred times the storage,
> kept the compiled result / and let the rest go.

**CUT** — Hard cut on "go". No air.

## Section one — two discs and a tape — 62s

**SHOT** — Medium, as the cold open. B-roll over the second half.

> On Thursday EA put twenty-five of its own PC classics on Steam and GOG. Nine
> mainline Ultima games, both Underworlds, Road Rash, Crusader, Theme Hospital,
> a stack of Wing Commander. Seven of them are badged Anniversary Editions /
> and the restoration on those seven was done by pulling the cinematics out of
> the company's own videotape archive.

> On Friday a small publisher called SNEG released Pool of Radiance: Ruins of
> Myth Drannor. A 2001 dungeon crawl / with a launch so broken it's still the
> reference anybody reaches for. No source code. No tools. No documentation.

> So the team worked backwards out of the retail builds. The discs. The object
> on the shelf. / And from that they got to something they could widen to four
> K, fit with an action bar, hang tooltips on, and sell for twenty dollars.
> They also had to clear the rights with three separate companies to do it.

**B-ROLL** — Steam store pages for the EA Classics set and for Pool of

## Section two — the third route — 66s

**SHOT** — Medium, unchanged. The cut is on the graphic, not the framing.

> Then on Sunday one of BioWare's co-founders put a number on the third route.
> Anyone who wants to buy Mass Effect off EA and finish what the studio started
> / should be budgeting north of two hundred million dollars.

> And that figure is the one I keep turning over, because I don't think it's
> mostly a figure about art. Mass Effect exists. The models exist, the writing
> exists, three shipped games and a remaster exist / and there are people
> walking around right now who know where every system in it is buried.

> Most of nine figures buys the work of getting all of that back into a state
> where a team that has never met it can pick it up. Refinding the tools.
> Documenting what was never documented. Rebuilding the content chain. Working
> out which of forty branches was the one. / And relearning by excavation what
> fifty people knew by Tuesday / and took with them when the badges went off.

**GRAPHIC** — `02-three-ways-back.svg` out on "two hundred million dollars",

**DELIVERY** — The four-item list is the only place he should sound tired. It

## Section three — what "no tools" means — 69s

**SHOT** — Push in slightly. Tighter than anything so far; this is the part he
knows from the inside.

> "No source code, no tools, no documentation" reads like negligence from
> outside. From in here it reads like a Thursday.

> Think about what a build from 2001 depended on. A custom exporter somebody
> wrote in a fortnight / to get geometry out of a package nobody licenses any
> more. A texture conversion step that lived as a batch file on one machine.
> Middleware with a version number nobody wrote down / because at the time
> everyone knew which version it was.

> A lightmap bake that reproduces on exactly one build of one tool, and shows
> you seams on every other. Source art at working resolution, which went when
> the drive filled / because the drive always filled, and the shipped
> compressed copy was right there.

> That's what a pipeline is while it's alive. A set of agreements held in
> people's heads and in the layout of a network share. / And the shipped build
> is the only thing anybody ever checks for completeness.

**GRAPHIC** — `03-what-no-tools-means.svg`. Draw the stages left to right over
20 frames, in on "what a build from 2001 depended on". The mark on "Archive"
lands late, on the word "completeness", and holds 3 seconds.

**DELIVERY** — The list is said as one breath group per item, slightly faster
than the rest of the video. It should sound like someone reciting things he has
personally lost.

## Section four — the handover — 48s

**SHOT** — Hold the tighter size. No change; the push stays spent.

> We hand over the thing that runs. In fifteen years nobody has once asked me
> to hand over the thing that rebuilds.

> And here's where I'll turn it on my own trade, because every item on that
> list is a technical artist's job. Version-pinned tools. Bakes that reproduce
> on a machine you've never seen. Source files a stranger can open. Naming
> nobody has to decode.

> All of it is the first budget line struck when a schedule compresses / and
> the reason is simple enough. None of it shows up in a build review. You
> cannot screenshot a working pipeline.

> Dispersal is the expensive part. And dispersal is a decision somebody makes
> on a spreadsheet in an afternoon.

## Section six — eighty-seven percent — 50s

**SHOT** — Medium, one step wider on the cut.

> And here's why none of this is sentiment. Capcom's last half-year moved
> thirty-six point eight six million games. Thirty-two point one eight million
> of them were catalogue. Things released in earlier years, some of them more
> than a decade back. / Eighty-seven percent. Seven units in eight that the
> company shipped in six months were games it had already finished.

> That is the business. The majority of the volume, in the biggest half the
> company has ever had.

> So the old work is where the money is / and the condition the old work is
> kept in belongs to nobody. Those two facts sit in the same annual report and
> they never meet.

## Outro — 34s

**SHOT** — Wide, back in the room. Warmer than anything before it.

> The thing that gets me is that film already ran this experiment. It burned
> its own negatives / and then spent fifty years, and a great deal of money,
> paying to undo it. We're still at the stage of treating the shipped build as
> the archive.

> Archive the project. The build is what you sold once. The project is the only
> thing that lets you sell it again.

> It's written up on gianlucascattarella dot I T, and there's a new one every
> Monday.

**DELIVERY** — The film line is the lighter note; let it sound amused. The two
lines after it drop back to flat.

**CUT** — Cut to black on "Monday". No end card, no outro music, no second
subscribe reminder.
