# The Monday editorial — voice

Everything in `article-voice.md` applies here too — first person, the
complicit "you", contractions, the banned construction, the sourcing rule.
This file is only what the column has on top of that.

## Non-negotiable: the column name is fixed, the headline is not

Every Monday editorial carries this line in its frontmatter, character for
character, and it never changes:

```
column: Architectures of the Void — the Monday editorial
```

That is the masthead. It renders as an eyebrow above the headline, the way
"In the box" sits above a piece on laroma24.

Two more fixed things, both non-negotiable:

```
category: Editorial
cover: /img/blog/editorial/cover.jpg
```

`Editorial` is its own category, so the column filters separately from the news
and gets its own colour. **Do not draw an SVG cover for the editorial** — the
column has one permanent illustration and every Monday uses it. The site forces
that image for anything in the Editorial category regardless of what the
frontmatter says, so a wrong `cover:` line cannot break the page; it just wastes
the run's time. Write the line above and move on.

**The `title:` is different every single week** and belongs to that week's
argument alone. Never put "Architectures of the Void" in the title — it is
already on the page, and repeating it would waste the one line search engines
read. The headline should carry the week's subject, because that is what
somebody is searching for.

Good: `title: Gamescom week, and the corridor we keep rebuilding`
Wrong: `title: Architectures of the Void — Gamescom week`

They are Italian. The blog is English. **Transpose the register, do not
translate it** — English has its own version of this voice (the columnist who
argues in public and doesn't hedge). What must survive the crossing is the
posture, not the idioms.

## The seven things all four pieces do

**1. The first sentence is a position, not a setup.**
No context paragraph, no "this week at Gamescom". They open by asserting
something, usually slightly against the grain, and spend the rest of the piece
earning it. *"Il problema non è che dal 2028 Sony non supporterà più il formato
fisico, sono i servizi a non essere all'altezza."* The reader knows within one
line what is being argued.

**2. Section headings are fragments, not descriptions.**
"Do ut des". "Giocare in cravatta". "Gambe deboli". "È la fine?". Two or three
words, nominal, ironic, sometimes a question. Never a summary of the section
below it. If a heading could be swapped into another article without anyone
noticing, it is wrong.

**3. Rhythm is deliberately uneven.**
Two-line paragraphs against eight-line blocks. Sentence fragments used as
punctuation. A long argued passage, then four words on their own line. The
unevenness is what makes it read like speech.

**4. First person, singular and plural, doing different jobs.**
The singular carries the opinion — *"a mio modo di vedere"*. The plural drags
the reader into a shared condition — *"siamo già dentro fino al collo"*. The
plural is the more powerful of the two and the one that gets underused.

**5. Verdicts land flat, with no cushion.**
*"Il digitale ha già vinto."* *"Amenità."* No "arguably", no "it could be said
that", no closing hedge that takes back the paragraph. Where there is doubt, it
is stated as doubt in its own sentence rather than smeared over the claim.

**6. Irony works by deflation, not by jokes.**
A collector's shelf becomes *"confezioni di plastica colorata"*, then
*"cartone come quello che usa Ikea"*. The technique is to describe the
prestigious thing in the plainest possible physical terms until it looks
ridiculous. Comparison does the work; no punchline is needed.

**7. The ending is a principle or a provocation, never a summary.**
*"Una rivoluzione tecnologica non è davvero una rivoluzione se rende la vita
migliore soltanto a chi la vende."* One line, wider than the article, left
standing. It does not recap. It does not say "in conclusion".

## How that becomes Gianluca's voice specifically

The reference writers are consumer columnists. He is not. He is a 3D
environment and technical artist, and that is the whole advantage — the same
posture from inside the industry rather than outside it.

So:

- **Argue from production, not from consumer grievance.** When a decision looks
  stupid from the outside, ask what it cost the people who had to build it. That
  is the angle nobody else in this register has.
- **The "we" is other artists**, not other buyers. "We" are the people who get
  handed the schedule after the trailer has been shown.
- **Technical detail is the evidence, never the subject.** Name the real thing —
  lightmaps, LOD chains, vertical slices, cloth sim, memory budgets — but always
  in service of the argument. If a paragraph is teaching, it has drifted.
- **Attack decisions and companies. Never named individuals.** Prices, policies,
  strategy, publisher behaviour: fair game, hard. A named executive or developer:
  off limits. He may want to work with these studios.

## First-person material

Gianluca supplies real episodes. **Ask him for two or three before writing each
editorial** — that is the arrangement he chose.

Never invent a specific experience for him: no clients, studios, projects,
conferences or hardware he owns. Generic craft experience true of anyone in the
job is fine and wanted. Where a real anecdote would clearly land and none has
been supplied, leave a visible `[[ANEDDOTO: what would go here]]` marker for him
to fill before approval rather than making one up.

## Shape

- 900–1400 words. Long enough to argue, short enough to be read in one sitting.
- Four to six sections.
- Two or three subjects from the week, connected by a thread — not a list.
  The connection between them is the piece. If they cannot be connected, pick
  fewer subjects.
- Not a roundup. Not "what's good and what's bad". A single argument that uses
  the week's news as its evidence.

## Warmer than the news

The news pieces ration emotion and spend it only when something genuinely
matters. The column is where it is spent: this is the one place he is
writing to be felt as well as understood, and a Monday that reads as level
as a Tuesday news piece has failed at the thing it exists for.

It is still the same two emotions — admiration for how something was made,
irritation at how it was decided. Here they are allowed to show.
